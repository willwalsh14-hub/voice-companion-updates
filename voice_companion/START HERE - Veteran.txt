VOICE COMPANION USER GUIDE

Guide version: 0.2.159-test.
Use this guide to learn Voice Companion, change its settings, and find help. Voice Companion speaks its own menus and messages. You can use your voice, your keyboard, or both. You do not need Microsoft Word or a screen reader to use its tools.
This is a test version. Some websites, email services, and audio streams may not work as expected. Read each message before you send, delete, or change anything important.

1. GETTING STARTED

1.1 Install Voice Companion
Use the file named VoiceCompanion-Setup.exe. This is the installer. It puts the app and its guides on your computer.
1. Open the installer.
2. Follow its prompts.
3. On the last page, leave Open Voice Companion now checked if you want to start the app.
4. Choose Finish.
You can open the app later from its desktop icon or the Windows Start menu. Use the installed app, not an older copy from a download folder.

1.2 Open the app and wake it
1. Open Voice Companion.
2. Wait while it checks for updates.
3. If it offers an update, say "Yes" or "No". You can also use the arrow keys and Enter.
4. When it says it is ready, say "Wake up", or press Down Arrow.
5. Wait for the main menu. Say the name of the tool you want.
For example, say "Word processor", "Notepad", "Email", "Web browser", "Radio", or "Podcasts".
The keyboard works while voice commands are asleep. Press Down Arrow to hear a menu item. Press Enter to open it.
To hear the version you are using, say "What version?", or press Control+Shift+V on the main menu.

1.3 Use the main menu
The main menu has these choices:
- File manager
- Word processor
- Notepad
- Email
- Web browser
- Media
- Options
- Check for updates
- Help
- Shut down the computer
- Restart the computer
- Exit Voice Companion
To move, say "Next" or "Previous", or press Down Arrow or Up Arrow.
To open the item you hear, say "That one", "Okay", or "Confirm", or press Enter.
To reach the first or last item, say "Start of list" or "End of list", or press Home or End.
You hear "Start of list" or "End of list" if you try to move past the first or last item. The list does not wrap around.
To find an item, say its name, or press its first letter. Then press Enter to open it. Text boxes use those keys for typing instead.
On the main menu, Alt says that you are in the main menu. On most other screens, press Alt to open a menu of that screen's actions. Use arrows and Enter to choose. Each item says its key and voice command. Press a number to open a group or choose an item. Type a letter to find an item. Escape goes back one level. Press Alt again to close the menu.

1.4 Set up your microphone
Voice Companion uses the microphone selected in Windows. You can use a built-in microphone or a working headset.
A headset helps keep the app's speech out of the microphone. It can also make your own voice easier to hear.
If voice commands do not work, check the microphone in Windows sound settings. Check that apps can use it. Then close and reopen Companion.
You can keep using the keyboard if the microphone is not available.

2. USING VOICE COMMANDS

2.1 Give a command
Say a command, then wait for the reply. For example, say "Help", or press F1, to open the guide.
In a menu, say "Next", then "That one" to choose the item you hear. Or press Down Arrow, then Enter.
To return to the start, say "Main menu", or press Escape until you hear Main menu.
Names in examples are sample names. Replace them with your own document, contact, folder, or favorite name.
This guide gives each task as a spoken command, then a key. For example: say "Help", or press F1. The key does the same thing as the spoken command.

2.2 Speak text or give commands
In Normal mode, you can give commands and dictate text. In a document, draft, note, or web text box, ordinary speech adds text.
Say "Commands only" when you want to move around without adding text by mistake.
Say "Dictation only" when you want your speech to become text. In this mode, some words that are usually commands become text.
Say "Normal mode" to return to voice commands and text entry. To choose a mode with the keyboard, press Alt+O, then choose Input options.
To hear the current mode, say "What mode?", or press Control+Shift+M on the main menu.
You can still leave a document, cancel a draft, or put Companion to sleep in dictation-only mode. Some editing commands also remain available.
If you are unsure which mode is active, switch to Normal mode before giving an editing or reading command.

2.3 Enter words that sound like commands
Say "Type literally", followed by the words you want to enter as text. For example, "Type literally new line" writes the words new line.
Without that prefix, "New line" makes a line break. "New paragraph" starts a new paragraph.
Say punctuation names while you speak. For example, "Hello comma Sam period" writes Hello, Sam.
Listen to the result. Speech recognition can choose the wrong word or punctuation mark.
The app treats typed words as text. Typing the word period does not add a period mark.

2.4 Type a voice command
1. Press Alt+T to reach the command box.
2. Type the command. For example, type list documents.
3. Press Enter.
Use this path when you prefer the keyboard or a voice command is not heard correctly.
In a web page, Alt+T lets you type an app command. Press Escape to return to page reading.

2.5 Answer Yes or No
When Companion asks a question, listen to the item name and the action first.
To do the action, say "Yes", or press Alt+Y. To stop it, say "No" or "Cancel", or press Alt+N or Escape.
You can also use arrows or Tab to choose Yes or No, then press Enter.
At a Yes or No prompt, Home selects Yes and End selects No.

3. USING HELP AND LESSONS

3.1 Open a Help topic
To open the guide inside the app, say "Help", or press F1 on the main menu. To choose a topic, say "Next", "Previous", and "That one", or use the arrows and Enter.
You can go straight to a topic. For example, say "Help with documents", "Help with email", or "Help with web".
Other useful requests are "Help with radio", "Help with podcasts", "Help with presets", and "Help with commands".
F1 on most screens opens the Help topic for that screen. Home and End reach the ends of a list.
Press Alt to open the help menu. It has Help topics, Subsections, Release notes, Lessons, the guide folder, Close help, and Main menu.

3.2 Read part of a topic
Each Help topic has a list of smaller parts. Choose the part you want, then listen.
Help reads one complete paragraph or list item at a time. For the next block, say "Continue reading", or press Down Arrow. Say "Read from top" to start that part again.
To move by paragraph, say "Next paragraph" or "Previous paragraph", or press Down Arrow or Up Arrow.
To move by sentence, say "Next sentence" or "Previous sentence", or press Control+Down Arrow or Control+Up Arrow.
To move by word, say "Next word" or "Previous word", or press Control+Right Arrow or Control+Left Arrow.
For the first or last block, say "First block" or "Last block", or press Home or End. Long paragraphs are read in full, even when they wrap across several screen lines.
Choosing another Help item stops the old speech before the next item is read.
Press Control to silence speech. To return to the smaller parts, say "Back to subsections", or press Escape.
For the full topic list, say "Back to topics", or press Escape again. To return to your work, say "Close help", or press Escape from the topic list.

3.3 Start the audio tutorial
The tutorial explains how to use the app. It waits for you between lessons. The tutorial explains the commands you give. It does not use them to send mail or change your files.
1. Return to the main menu.
2. Say "Start tutorial", or press Control+Shift+T on the main menu.
3. Say a course name, such as "Email" or "Web browser". You can also use arrows and Enter.
4. Listen to the first lesson.
5. When you are ready, say "Next lesson", or press Down Arrow.
Courses cover Getting started, Word processor, Notepad, Email, Web browser, Radio, Podcasts, Options, and Help and updates.

3.4 Find, repeat, and leave a lesson
To hear the same lesson, say "Repeat", or press Control+Shift+R. To go back, say "Previous lesson", or press Up Arrow.
For the lesson names, say "Lesson list", or press Control+L. Use arrows and Enter to choose one.
To find a lesson, say "Find lesson" followed by a topic, such as "Find lesson favorites". Or press Control+F, type the topic, and press Enter.
For the course's command list, say "List commands", or press F1.
To learn about a command, say "Explain command", followed by the command. For example, say "Explain command send it". Or press Control+Shift+F, type the command, and press Enter.
If a long explanation pauses, say "Continue reading", or press Control+Down Arrow.
To go back one level, say "Go back", or press Escape. Say "Courses" to return to the course list.
To leave the tutorial, say "Main menu" or "Close tutorial", or press Escape from the course list.
Press Alt to open the lessons menu. It has the lesson list, Repeat, Find lesson, Explain a command, and the other lesson commands.

4. SPEECH AND VOICES

4.1 Stop or pause speech
Press Control to silence Companion. This works while the microphone is asleep too.
By voice, say "Be quiet", "Stop talking", "Pause", or "Shut up". Say "Resume" or "Keep talking" to continue paused speech.
Control stops speech instead of pausing it. To hear the text again, use a reading key, such as Down Arrow.
Say "Next" or "Previous" to interrupt a spoken menu item and move. These commands also work while Help or a lesson is being read. A new command can stop the old reading and do something else. For example, say "Next message" while mail is being read.
For audio such as radio or podcasts, say "Pause audio" or "Stop audio" to make your meaning clear. Or press Control+P to pause or resume.

4.2 Change speed, volume, or pitch
Say "Faster" or "Slower" to change speech speed. Say "Louder", "Quieter", or "Softer" to change volume.
To set a value, say "Rate" or "Speed", followed by a number. For example, say "Rate four" or "Speed four". For volume, say "Volume", followed by a number, such as "Volume fifty".
Speech rate runs from minus ten to ten. Zero is the normal speed. Volume runs from zero to one hundred.
Pitch changes how high or low a voice sounds. Say "Higher pitch" or "Lower pitch" to change it one step at a time. To set a value, say "Pitch", followed by a number. For example, say "Pitch minus three".
Pitch runs from minus ten to ten. Speech settings do not change text size or music volume.
From the keyboard, press Alt+O. Open Voice options. Use Tab to reach a setting, then use arrows to change it. Choose OK to save.

4.3 Choose a voice
1. Say "Voice list" or "List voices".
2. Choose the speech system you want. The list calls this a synthesizer.
3. Choose a voice from that system.
4. Listen to the sample and confirm your choice.
SAPI means the voices provided by Windows. eSpeak has US and UK English choices. AI voices need a separate setup and an internet connection.
Use "Next voice", "Previous voice", and "Okay" to choose. Say "Cancel voice" to keep your earlier choice.
With the keyboard, press Alt+O and choose Synthesizer options. Tab to the voice list, choose with the arrows, then choose OK.
The available voices depend on what is installed and set up on your computer.

4.4 Use an AI voice
An AI voice reads some app text through an online service. Your helper must set up access before you can use it.
Open Options, then Synthesizer options. Your helper can enter the service key in the private AI API key box. This key is a code that gives the app access to the speech service.
The Allow narration text to be sent to OpenAI checkbox lets you permit online speech. Check it only if you agree to send the text, then save the setting.
Say "Use AI voice" after setup. Say "Use SAPI" or "Use eSpeak" to use local speech instead.
An AI voice needs internet access and may use paid service credit. Long reading may take longer to start.
The app uses a local voice for typing and menu movement so you can hear each key promptly. If online speech fails, Companion uses local speech.

5. READING AND EDITING TEXT

5.1 Read from your current place
These commands work in documents, email, web pages, and notes where text reading is available.
To start at the beginning, say "Read from top" or "Start reading". In a document, draft, note, or the Document reader, you can also press Control+Shift+R.
For the next part, say "Continue reading", or press Down Arrow.
To move by letter or symbol, say "Next character" or "Previous character", or press Right Arrow or Left Arrow.
To move by word, say "Next word" or "Previous word", or press Control+Right Arrow or Control+Left Arrow.
To move by line, say "Next line" or "Previous line", or press Down Arrow or Up Arrow.
To move by sentence, say "Next sentence" or "Previous sentence". In Help, press Control+Down Arrow or Control+Up Arrow. In a document, press Alt, 5, then Q or R.
To move by paragraph, say "Next paragraph" or "Previous paragraph", or press Control+Down Arrow or Control+Up Arrow. In Help, press Down Arrow or Up Arrow.
To reach the start or end of a line, say "Go to start of line" or "Go to end of line", or press Home or End.
A line may end at a line break in the text. It may differ from a line shown on the screen.

5.2 Read with the keyboard
Left Arrow and Right Arrow move by character. Control+Left Arrow and Control+Right Arrow move by word.
In documents and email, Up Arrow and Down Arrow move by line. Control+Up Arrow and Control+Down Arrow move by paragraph.
In Help, Up Arrow reads the previous paragraph or list item. Down Arrow reads the next one. Home and End reach the first and last blocks in the topic.
In documents, email, and web pages, Home and End reach the start or end of a line. Control+Home and Control+End reach the start or end of the text.
In web pages, Page Up and Page Down move a longer distance. Page reading starts from the place you last chose.
The same keys can review a received email. You can read and copy received text, but you cannot rewrite that message.

5.3 Select text
Selected text is the part you have chosen to copy, change, or delete.
From the keyboard, hold Shift while pressing a movement key. Control+A selects all the text in an open text view.
With your voice, say "Select word", "Select line", "Select sentence", "Select character", or "Select paragraph" for the current part.
For all the text, say "Select all", or press Control+A. To check your choice, say "What is selected" or "Read selection". In a document, you can also press Insert+Shift+Down Arrow.
In a document or draft, say "Select", followed by the part you want. You can use first, next, previous, last, current, or a number with character, word, line, sentence, or paragraph. For example, say "Select first paragraph", "Select next paragraph", "Select previous paragraph", or "Select paragraph 3".
In an email address box, a subject box, a note, or a web text box, selection applies only to the text in that area.
Web page reading text is read-only. To change a web answer, enter its text box first.

5.4 Choose named words
To select a word or phrase, say "Select", followed by the words you want. For example, say "Select red fox". In a document, you can also press Control+Alt+S, type the words, and press Enter.
To select a passage, say "Select", followed by the start word, "through", and the end word. For example, say "Select cat through dog". You can use a phrase at either end, such as "Select red fox through sleeps".
You can also mark each end in a separate command. Say "Selection start at", followed by the start word or phrase. Then say "Selection end at", followed by the end word or phrase. For example, say "Selection start at cat", then "Selection end at dog".
If the words appear more than once, Companion reads each matching place. Say "Next" or "Previous" to hear another match.
Say "Read sentence" to hear the nearby sentence again. To choose, say "That one", "Confirm that", or "Okay", or press Enter. Arrows also move through the matches.
To choose a match by number, say "Match", followed by its number. For example, say "Match 3". You can also say "First match" or "Second match". To leave the text as it is, say "Cancel selection", or press Escape.
To select the word all rather than all the text, say "Select the word all".

5.5 Copy, cut, paste, or remove text
Say "Copy selection", "Cut selection", or "Paste". From the keyboard, use Control+C, Control+X, or Control+V.
In a document or draft, say "Copy", "Cut", or "Delete", followed by the words you want to change. For example, say "Copy cat", "Cut cat", or "Delete cat".
Say "Delete word", "Delete line", "Delete sentence", or "Delete paragraph" to remove the current part. In a document, these are also in the Edit menu: press Alt, then 2.
Say "Scratch" or "Scratch that", or press Control+Shift+Z, to remove selected text. If no text is selected, the command removes the most recent dictated text where this feature is supported.
In documents and draft message bodies, say "Undo", or press Control+Z, to reverse an edit. Check the text after the change.
Moving to a new place clears the old selection. Check your choice again before you change or remove text.

5.6 Replace or insert words
To replace text, say "Replace", followed by the old words, "with", and the new words. For example, say "Replace cat with dog". Or press Control+H, type the same words, and press Enter. You can also say "Change", followed by the old words, "to", and the new words, such as "Change cat to dog".
If the app finds one match, it changes that text at once. If it finds several matches, use "Next", "Previous", and "That one" to choose the right place.
To stop without changing the words, say "Cancel replacement", or press Escape. Say a punctuation name if you want it in the new text.
For example, say "Replace cat with dog period" to use dog followed by a period. A period that speech recognition adds on its own does not become part of the replacement.
To replace the current word, sentence, or paragraph, say "Replace word with", "Replace sentence with", or "Replace paragraph with", followed by the new text. For example, say "Replace word with hello", "Replace sentence with Thank you", or "Replace paragraph with I will call tomorrow".
To choose where to add text, say "Insert before" or "Insert after", followed by a word or phrase already in the text. For example, say "Insert before red fox". In a document, you can also press Alt, 2, then O or P.
Choose a matching place if asked. Then speak the words to add. In Commands only, use "Type literally" before the new words.

6. WRITE A DOCUMENT

6.1 Create and name a document
Choose Word processor for its menu. Create a document starts a blank file. Open a document starts at drives. Recent documents lists files used in the app. Options opens Word processor options. Control+N creates a file. Control+O opens one. Control+R opens recent files from this menu.
The Word processor creates files that Microsoft Word can open. You do not need to have Word installed. For help, say "Help with documents", or press F1 in the Word processor menu.
1. Say "Create a document". Or choose Word processor, then press Control+N.
2. Say "Name document", followed by the file name you want, such as "Name document My Letter". Or press F2, type the name, and press Enter.
3. Speak a short sentence or type into the document.
4. To check what you wrote, say "Read paragraph", or press Alt, 5, then B.
The file name and the title inside your letter are separate. Naming the file does not add a title to the page.

6.2 Write and move through the document
To start a new paragraph, say "New paragraph", or press Control+Enter. To start a line in the same paragraph, say "New line".
To review your text, say "Read paragraph", "Spell word", "Next word", or "Next sentence". The keys are in chapter 5.
To move to an end, say "Go to beginning of document" or "Go to end of document", or press Control+Home or Control+End.
You can also say "Move to beginning of document", "Move to end of document", "Jump to beginning of document", or "Jump to end of document".
After moving, new words are added at that place. A previous selection is cleared.
You can type directly too. Enter creates a paragraph. Use the reading and editing keys in chapter 5. To give any document command with the keyboard, press Alt to open the document menu. See part 6.9.

6.3 Change the look of text
Choose the text first if you want to change only part of it. With no selection, many commands change the current paragraph.
Say "Make this bold", "Make this italic", or "Underline this", or press Control+B, Control+I, or Control+U. These keys turn the style on or off.
You can also say "Remove bold", "Remove italic", or "Remove underline" to turn a style off.
To format specific words, say "Bold", "Italicize", "Underline", or "Highlight", followed by those words. For example, say "Bold cat", "Italicize cat", "Underline cat", or "Highlight cat".
To choose a font, say "Set font to", followed by its name. For example, say "Set font to Arial". Or press Control+Shift+F, type the name, and press Enter.
To choose a font size, say "Set font size to", followed by a number and "points". For example, say "Set font size to 14 points". Or press Control+Shift+P, type the size, and press Enter.
To set both, say "Set font", followed by the font name, the size, and "points". For example, say "Set font Times New Roman 16 points".
To change just the current word, say "Bold word", "Italic word", or "Underline word". To change its size, say "Set word size to", followed by the number and "points". For example, say "Set word size to 14 points".
To change the current word's font, say "Set word font to", followed by the font name. For example, say "Set word font to Arial". "Remove bold from word" turns bold off for that word.
To hear the text settings, say "Say font" or "Read formatting", or press Insert+F. "Say font name", "Say size", "Say bold", "Say italic", and "Say underline" read one setting.
If selected text has more than one setting, Companion tells you that the setting is mixed.

6.4 Use headings and align text
A heading names a part of your document. Heading levels run from 1 to 6.
To apply a heading, say "Heading", followed by a level number, such as "Heading 1". Or press Control+Alt and the number, such as Control+Alt+1. Use a higher number for a smaller heading.
"Make this a heading" uses level 1. To remove the heading style, say "Normal text", or press Control+Shift+N.
To place a paragraph on the page, say "Align left", "Align center", "Align right", or "Align justified". Or press Control+L, Control+E, Control+R, or Control+J.
Justified text lines up with both page edges. Say "Say alignment" or "Say heading" to check the setting.
With selected text, these commands apply to the selected paragraphs.

6.5 Change line spacing
Line spacing is the space between the lines in a paragraph.
To set the spacing, say "Single space" or "Double space", or press Control+1 or Control+2. For one and a half, say "Line spacing 1.5", or press Control+5.
For another value, say "Line spacing", followed by the number. Or press Alt, 3, then I, and type the number.
For another value, say "Custom spacing". Give the value when asked, such as "One point seven five" or "Exactly eighteen points".
Companion repeats the value. Say "Yes" to use it, "No" to give another value, or "Cancel" to stop.
To change spacing throughout the document, say "Set document line spacing to", followed by the value. For example, say "Set document line spacing to 1.25".
Say "Read line spacing" for the current paragraph. Say "Read document line spacing" for the whole document.
"Reset line spacing" resets the current paragraph. "Reset document line spacing" resets all paragraphs. These spacing choices are also in the Format menu: press Alt, then 3.
A new paragraph keeps the spacing of the paragraph before it.

6.6 Make a list
1. Say "Bulleted list" or "Numbered list", or press Control+Shift+L or Control+Shift+7.
2. Speak or type the first item.
3. For the next item, say "New paragraph", or press Enter.
4. To return to ordinary text, say "End list", or press Alt, 4, then F.
A bulleted list marks each item with a dot. A numbered list gives each item a number.

6.7 Make a table
1. Say "Create table with", the number of rows, "rows and", the number of columns, and "columns". For example, say "Create table with two rows and two columns". Or press Control+Alt+T and type the size.
2. Speak the text for the first cell. A cell is one box in the table.
3. To move to the next box, say "Next cell", or press Tab.
4. To check your work, say "Read table", or press Alt, 8, then A.
You can also say "Previous cell", "Next row", "Previous row", "Next column", and "Previous column". The keys are Shift+Tab, Alt+Down Arrow, Alt+Up Arrow, Alt+Right Arrow, and Alt+Left Arrow.
Say "Read cell" to hear the current cell. "Clear cell" removes its words.
"Add row" and "Add column" make the table larger. "Delete row" and "Delete column" remove those parts.
Say "Leave table" to write after the table. "Delete table" removes the table. Say "Undo" at once if that was a mistake. All table commands are in the Table menu: press Alt, then 8.
The editor makes simple tables with straight rows and columns. It may not open more complex tables from other programs.

6.8 Save, copy, close, or reopen a document
Companion saves as you write. To save under the current name, say "Save document", or press Control+S.
To keep a second file, say "Save as", followed by its new name. For example, say "Save as My Letter Copy". Or press Control+Shift+S and type the name. The first file stays in place.
To rename the file, say "Name document", followed by the new name. For example, say "Name document My Letter". Say "Undo" right after a rename to restore the earlier name.
When you are done, say "Leave document" or "Go back", or press Control+W or Escape. Companion asks whether to save.
Say "Yes" to keep the current file name, or to name an untitled file. Say "No" to discard changes made since you last opened, saved, or named the file.
Press Escape at the save or name prompt to keep editing. Check the file name Companion reads before leaving.
Say "Resume document" to return to the current document while Companion remains open.
After reopening the app, say "List documents", then choose a file. Or choose Word processor, then press Control+R for recent files or Control+O to open one. To open a file by name, say "Open document", followed by the saved name, such as "Open document My Letter".
Say "Open documents" to open the document folder in Windows. "Where are my documents?" reads its location.
Files are kept in Documents, inside the Voice Companion folder. They use the .docx file type.
Companion can reopen the simple files it creates. It may refuse a complex Word file to avoid changing it incorrectly.

6.9 Document command menu
In a document, press Alt for File, Edit, Format, Insert, Read and navigate, Dictation, Table, and More commands. Use arrows and Enter. Type a letter to find an item. Escape goes back a level, then back to the document. Each action uses the same rules as its voice command. If a value is needed, type or say it and press Enter. Escape cancels the prompt. Alt+T lets you type any voice command.
Control+S saves. Control+Shift+S saves as. F2 names the document. Control+O opens a file. Control+N creates one. Control+W closes it after a save prompt. Control+Z undoes. Control+A selects all. Control+C copies, Control+X cuts, and Control+V pastes. Control+F finds text. Control+H replaces text; enter the old words followed by with and the new words. Control+B, Control+I, and Control+U toggle bold, italic, and underline. Control+L, Control+E, Control+R, and Control+J set left, center, right, and full alignment. Control+Shift+F chooses a font. Control+Shift+P chooses its size. Control+1, Control+2, and Control+5 set single, double, and one-and-a-half spacing. Control+Enter makes a new paragraph. Control+Shift+R reads the document. F4 starts dictation. Shift+F4 pauses it. F1 opens help. Alt+O opens Word processor options. In a table, Tab and Shift+Tab move among cells. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move among rows. Alt+Left and Alt+Right move among columns.

Use "Commands only" to review without adding speech as text. Say "Normal mode" when you want to write again.

7.3 Open a saved note

8. SET UP EMAIL

8.1 Connect an email account
Connect an account before relying on Companion to send or organize email.
1. Say "Add account". Or press Alt+O, choose Email options, then choose Add an email account.
2. Choose Gmail, Outlook or Exchange, Yahoo, or Thunderbird as your email service. Say its name, or press G, O, Y, or T, then Enter.
3. Follow the sign-in prompts. Ask your helper if you need help with the sign-in page.
4. Open the inbox and send a test message to someone you know.
Google and Microsoft use their usual sign-in pages. Work or school accounts may need your organization's approval.
Yahoo uses an app password, not your normal password. To make one, sign in to Yahoo, open Account security, and choose Generate app password. Copy it.
When you choose Yahoo, Companion asks for your email address. Type it or say it, then press Enter.
Next, type or paste the app password, then press Enter. Press Control+V to paste. For privacy, do not say it aloud. You hear "Hidden character" for each letter.
Companion checks the password with Yahoo. It tells you if it worked. If not, it tells you what to do. If Yahoo stops accepting a saved app password, make a new one. Then say "Add account" and choose Yahoo again. The new password replaces the old one. Say "Change address" to fix the address. Press Escape or say "Cancel" to stop.
Thunderbird is a free email program. Companion uses the email accounts already set up in it. When you choose Thunderbird, Companion checks what is missing and tells you the next step. It may offer to open the Thunderbird website, install its connection in Thunderbird, or start Thunderbird. Say yes or no, or press Y or N. Your helper adds your email account in Thunderbird once. Keep Thunderbird open while you use email in Companion. It can stay minimized.
Enter passwords and codes privately. Do not read them aloud to someone you do not trust.
If account setup is not available, ask your helper to check the app's email setup.

8.2 Choose or remove an account
Say "Add another account" to connect a second address. Say "List email accounts" to hear the saved accounts.
To choose another account from a list, say "Switch account". Or press Alt, 6, then 2 in email.
You hear each account, such as "Gmail, sam@gmail.com, 2 of 3". The account in use is first. It is called current.
Use Up Arrow and Down Arrow, then press Enter. Or say "Next" and "That one". You can also say the account number, the service name, or the address. For example, say "2", "Yahoo", or "sam at gmail dot com".
Companion switches to that account and opens its inbox. Press Escape or say "Cancel" to keep the account you have.
You can also say "Switch to", followed by the email service name. For example, say "Switch to Yahoo" if only one Yahoo account is connected.
With two accounts of the same type, say "Switch to" followed by the full address.
To hear the account in use, say "Which email account?". Check it before sending mail.
With the keyboard, press Alt+O and choose Email options. Sending account names the account in use. Choose another account there to switch.
"Check Gmail", "Check Outlook", and "Check Yahoo" open that account when exactly one of its type is connected.
To remove an account from Companion, say "Remove email account". Or press Alt, 6, then 4 in email.
1. Choose the account from the list, the same way as above.
2. Listen to the question. It names the account.
3. Say "Yes" to remove it, or "No" to keep it. Or press Y or N.
Nothing is removed until you say yes. Pressing Enter alone answers no.
Removing an account only removes its saved sign-in from Companion. Your email stays with your email service. Your other accounts do not change.
If you remove the account in use, Companion picks the next account and tells you which one.
For Gmail or Microsoft, you can also stop its access in your Google or Microsoft account. Open the account's security page, and remove Voice Companion from the apps list. A helper can assist.
For Yahoo, delete the app password in Yahoo Account security if you no longer need it.
For Thunderbird, removing only stops Companion from using that account. Thunderbird does not change.
To connect the account again later, say "Add account".
Accounts are also in the email menu. In email, press Alt, then 6. Choose Add account, Switch account, Which account, or Remove account.

8.3 Add and find contacts
A contact is a saved name and email address.
To save a contact, say "Add contact", followed by the person's name, "as", and their email address. For example, say "Add contact Bob as bob@example.com". Or press Control+Shift+K in the inbox. Type the name, the word as, and the address, then press Enter.
To hear saved names, say "List contacts", or press Control+K in email. Use arrows to hear each name. Press Enter to hear the address.
To find a contact, say "Find contact", followed by the saved name. For example, say "Find contact Bob". Use the saved contact's name when Companion asks who to email.
Each email account has its own contact list. Local contacts can be used without internet access.
When Gmail is selected, say "Connect Google Contacts" to request access to Google Contacts. Or press Alt+O, choose Email options, then choose Connect Google Contacts.
Approve that access only if you want to link those contacts. A helper can assist with the permission page.
Google Calendar and Google Drive are not connected in this version.

9. WRITE AN EMAIL

9.1 Start a new message
1. Say "Write an email" from the main menu, or "New message" while reading email.
2. Give the address or a saved contact name when asked.
3. Give the subject when asked.
4. Speak the message in short parts. Listen back before sending.
From the keyboard in email, press Control+N to start a new message. Type an address in To and press Enter. Continue through the subject and message.
When speaking an email address, say "at" for @ and "dot" for each period. For example, say "sam at example dot com" for sam@example.com. Say "underscore" for _ and "dash" for -.
Press Alt to open the email draft menu. It has Message, Fields, Format, and Contacts and help. Message has Send, Save draft, Read draft, Save and close, and Discard draft. While the menu is open, the keys you press choose items. They are not added to the message.

9.2 Change To, CC, BCC, or Subject
The To field holds the main recipient's address. CC sends a copy to another person. BCC sends a copy without showing that address to the other recipients.
To reach a part of the draft, say "Go to To", "Go to CC", "Go to BCC", "Go to Subject", or "Go to Body". Or press Tab and Shift+Tab.
Type the address or subject and press Enter. Left Arrow and Right Arrow review characters. Backspace and Delete remove characters.
To set the To field by voice, say "Email to", followed by the address. For example, say "Email to sam@example.com".
To set the subject, say "Subject is", followed by the subject text. For example, say "Subject is Hello from me".
Say "CC" or "BCC" to add a copy address. Say "Skip" if you do not want to add one.
"Read recipient", "Read subject", "Read CC", and "Read BCC" let you check those parts.

9.3 Write and format the message
Ordinary speech adds text to the message. "New paragraph" starts a paragraph. "Read paragraph" reads it back.
The text selection and formatting commands in chapters 5 and 6 work in the message body too. In the body, say "Toggle bold", "Toggle italic", or "Toggle underline", or press Control+B, Control+I, or Control+U.
You can change a font, text size, line spacing, heading, list, or table. Address and subject boxes use plain text.
The person reading your mail may see different fonts or spacing in their email app.
Use "Type literally" to write words that might be heard as commands. Use "Undo" to reverse an edit in the body. To hear the whole draft, say "Read email draft", or press Control+Shift+R. For help, say "Help with writing email", or press F1.

9.4 Save or cancel a draft
To keep a message for later, say "Save draft", or press Control+S. Say "List email drafts" to hear the saved drafts.
To reopen a saved draft, say "Open email draft". Or press Control+O in the inbox. Use arrows to choose a draft, then press Enter.
To open a draft by name, say "Open email draft", followed by its name, such as "Open email draft Untitled".
To leave an unsent draft and discard its changes, say "Cancel", "Cancel draft", or "Back to inbox", or press Escape.
These cancel commands work in a new message, reply, reply-all message, or forward. They work from each draft field.
Save the draft first if you want to keep it. "Leave email" saves the draft and returns to the main menu.
Canceling cannot take back a message already sent. If you reopened a saved draft, canceling keeps its earlier saved copy.

9.5 Review and send
1. Say "Send email", "Send it", or "Send that", or press Control+Enter or Alt+S in the draft.
2. Listen to the sending account, addresses, subject, and message.
3. If something is wrong, say "Cancel send", or press Alt+N. Fix it, then start the review again.
4. Say "Yes", or press Alt+Y, only when the review is right.
Nothing is sent before the review and confirmation. If no account is connected, Companion saves a draft instead of sending it.
After the email service accepts the message, Companion says it was sent. Check delivery with your helper during early tests.
If Companion says the result is uncertain, check your sent mail and ask the recipient before trying again.
With Yahoo, Companion also puts a copy in your Sent folder. If it cannot, it tells you. The message was still sent.

10.1 Read messages

2. Listen to the sender, subject, and date of the selected message.

To choose a message by number, say "Read message" or "Open message", followed by its number. For example, say "Read message 1" or "Open message 1".

10.3 Select, move, or delete messages

To choose a destination folder from a list, say "Move", or press Control+Shift+V. To name the folder directly, say "Move to", followed by its name, such as "Move to Saved".

10.4 Reply or forward

For a forward, enter the new address when asked. Speak your added text, then use the review and send steps in chapter 9.

10.5 Create and manage folders
To create a folder, say "Create folder", followed by the new name, such as "Create folder Receipts". Or press Control+Shift+E.

To rename a folder, say "Rename folder", or press F2, and choose it. Then say "Rename to", followed by the new name, such as "Rename to Bills". To give both names at once, say "Rename folder", the old name, "to", and the new name. For example, say "Rename folder Receipts to Bills".
To choose a folder and its new location, say "Move folder", or press Control+Shift+M. To give both names at once, say "Move folder", the folder name, "into", and the new parent folder name. For example, say "Move folder Bills into Archive".
To choose a folder to delete from a list, say "Delete folder", or press Control+Shift+D. To name the folder directly, say "Delete folder", followed by its name. For example, say "Delete folder Bills".
Listen to the full folder location and the action. Say "Yes" to go ahead or "No" to cancel.

10.6 Read links and load more mail
In email that keeps web-style formatting, Companion reads links and buttons as part of the message.

Say "Activate current link" or "Activate current button" to use the selected item. To choose by number, say "Open link" or "Open button", followed by its number. For example, say "Open link 1" or "Open button 1".
For another page of mail, say "Next messages" or "Previous messages", or press Page Down or Page Up in the message list. To refresh the folder, say "List messages", or press F5.
To reach the first or last message in the folder, say "First message" or "Last message", or press Home or End. Messages from another page may need to load first. For email help, say "Help with email", or press F1.
The Messages per list setting is in Email options. Choices include 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 100, 1000, and all.

11.10 Use website keys and sliders
Some websites have their own keys. For example, on YouTube, K plays or pauses a video. J and L skip back and ahead. M turns the sound off, and F fills the screen.

- It is also true for a search box that shows suggestions, such as the search box on YouTube.
- Arrow keys leave the box at its first or last line. Tab moves on to the next control.
Choice boxes and sliders work like this:
- When you arrow past a choice box or a slider, Companion only reads it. Its value never changes by accident.
- Press Enter to start using it. Tab onto it also starts using it.

18.4 Input and Email options
In Input options, you can choose Normal, Commands only, or Dictation only. The choice affects how spoken words are used.
Dictation type is a separate choice. Old school keeps the speech entry you used before. It is the default.
Choose AI-based to use the local AI speech model and light cleanup. The model adds stops and capital letters as you speak. Cleanup removes um, uh, and clear repeated starts. For example, "I I, um, want to go" becomes "I want to go".
Open Options with Alt+O. Choose Input options. Tab to Dictation type. Use the arrows to choose Old school or AI-based. Choose OK to save. Cancel keeps the old choice.
This choice works for spoken text in documents, notes, email, and web text boxes. Typed text and commands keep their normal role. Names, passwords, codes, and web addresses are not cleaned as prose. Say "Type literally" before words you want to keep as spoken.
AI-based works offline. It needs no account or extra fee. It adds no second online step. Text appears after a short pause at the end of each phrase. Speed depends on the PC. Keep phrases short. We have not set a fixed speed promise for live speech.
Cleanup does not fix every stumble or guess words you left out. It keeps grammar and meaning as spoken. It leaves "very, very" and "had had" alone. Read your text and use Undo if needed. If the AI model fails, the app tells you and uses basic offline speech.
Allow microphone audio to be sent to Azure for recognition is off by default. Turn it on only if you agree to send audio and your helper has set up that service. It stays off in web forms, settings, and AI-based dictation.

Automatic page reading controls whether new pages start reading aloud. It is on by default.
Companion can open HTTP links and save them in favorites and history. A bare address such as example.com still starts with HTTPS. To request HTTP directly, type the full address, such as http://example.com.
HTTP does not encrypt the connection. Guided voice review still requires HTTPS before it sends a form.
The Guided browser setting lets you choose embedded Edge or Chrome. Companion uses a separate browser profile for its pages.
- Embedded Edge is the usual choice. Say "Use embedded Edge". It opens a browser window owned by Companion and does not need the browser extension.
- Chrome uses Google Chrome on your PC. Say "Use Chrome". If Chrome is not installed, say "Set up Chrome".
- Edge, Brave, and Firefox are no longer guided browsers. If you chose one of them before, Companion now uses embedded Edge and tells you once.
Embedded Edge needs the Microsoft WebView2 component on the PC. If it cannot start, Companion uses Chrome and tells you. If Chrome is not installed either, Companion tells you to say "Set up Chrome".
Google does not allow signing in to a Google account in embedded Edge. For a site that needs Google sign-in, say "Use Chrome", or use your regular browser with the extension.
That means a site may ask you to sign in even if you already signed in in your usual browser.
Choose List website favorites to open the saved website list. New favorite actions are also in the web browser menu.

18.7 Radio and Podcasts options
Both categories have Keep audio playing in the background. It is off at first. With it on, audio keeps playing when you return to the main menu.
In Radio options, you can choose a Station database, List radio presets, or List scheduled recordings.
Station database chooses which station lists to search. Presets are saved stations. Scheduled recordings are future recordings.
In Podcasts options, choose a Podcast subscription, then set Automatic downloads for this subscription.
Choose manual, all, or a whole-number limit. List subscriptions opens the saved-show list.
The limit applies only to the chosen show.

18.8 Auto-update and Startup and exit options
In Auto-update options, you can turn Check automatically at startup on or off. Choose Check for updates now to check at any time.
In Startup and exit options, Start with Windows is off by default. Turn it on to open the installed app when you sign in to Windows.
The app starts in sleep mode and waits for "Wake up" or keyboard input. Uninstalling Companion removes that startup choice.
Ask before exiting Voice Companion, Ask before shutting down the computer, and Ask before restarting the computer are on by default.
Each setting controls its own Yes or No prompt. Document save questions still apply when you turn off an exit prompt.

19. SLEEP, EXIT, AND COMPUTER POWER

19.1 Put the microphone to sleep
To pause ordinary voice requests, say "Go to sleep", or press F12. To continue, say "Wake up", or press F12 again.
Your place in a document, email, or menu is kept. A note or draft is saved before sleep where needed.
If an email is waiting for your approval to send, that approval is canceled. Review the message again before sending it.
The keyboard still works while voice commands are asleep. Sleep is different from closing the app or putting Windows to sleep.

19.2 Close Companion
To close the app, say "Exit Companion" or "Close Companion", or press Alt+F4. You can also choose Exit Voice Companion on the main menu.
Escape at the main menu also requests exit.
At the exit prompt, No is selected by default. To exit, say "Yes", or press Alt+Y. To keep working, say "No", or press Alt+N.
If a document is open, answer its save question first. Escape cancels the exit request and keeps the document open.
Exiting Companion does not shut down Windows.

Companion says "Shutting down. Goodbye." and waits for the full message before closing.

19.3 Restart or shut down the computer
These choices affect the whole computer. Save your work in other apps first.
Choose Restart the computer or Shut down the computer on the main menu. You can also say "Restart computer" or "Shut down computer".
Companion asks a separate Yes or No question by default. Listen to the action, then answer.
If a document is open, Companion asks whether to save it first. Press Escape at that prompt to cancel the restart or shutdown.
Windows may ask about unsaved work in other apps. Companion does not force them to close.
The startup choice and the computer power choices are separate settings.

20. CHECK FOR UPDATES

20.1 Find a newer version
Say "Check for updates", or choose Check for updates on the main menu.
Companion also checks at startup when automatic checks are on. If an update is offered, say "Yes" to install or "No" to keep working.
A later update offer waits until you have returned to the main menu or a suitable pause in your work.
If the check fails, you can keep using the current version and try again later.

20.2 Install an update
1. Accept the update with Yes.
2. Let Companion save your current work and download the file.
3. Listen to the progress messages. Say "Status" or "Update status" to hear the current percentage.
4. Wait while Companion closes, installs the update, and opens again.
Say "Cancel update" while the download is in progress if you need to stop it. Tab and Shift+Tab move between Status and Cancel update. Enter activates the button. Alt+S or F5 reads status. Alt+C or Escape cancels the download.
The app checks the downloaded file before using it. Windows may still need your helper for a security prompt.
The app speaks progress at each five-percent step during download and installation. Say "Status" or "Update status" to hear just the current percentage. This works while a progress message is being read.
If Windows cannot open the microphone for the update window, the window stays open and keeps trying. Press the Status button or Enter to hear progress.
During installation, a separate update window appears. Its microphone listens for "Status" or "Update status". You can also press the Status button or Enter to hear progress.
When setup finishes, you hear "Installation complete, restarting Voice Companion." The full message plays before the app restarts. Companion opens in sleep mode. Say "Wake up" or use the keyboard when you are ready. If Windows takes time to release the microphone, the app tries to open it again.
Your account data and documents stay in their normal folders. The installer also refreshes the guides.
Companion does not force Windows to restart. It tells you if a restart is needed.

20.3 Read the release notes
Release notes explain what changed in a version and how to use the changes.
Say "What's new" or "Release notes". Or press F1 on the main menu, press End to reach What's new, then press Enter.
Use "Continue reading", "Next paragraph", "Previous paragraph", and the other reading commands to review them.
To return to your task, say "Close help", or press Escape until Help closes. The notes identify the current version and the earlier version used for comparison.

21. FIND YOUR GUIDE FILES

21.1 Open the guide folder
Say "Open user guides" or "Open guides folder", or press Control+Shift+G on the main menu.
You can also use the Voice Companion User Guides shortcut on the desktop or in the Start menu.
Open Documents, then the Voice Companion folder, then User Guides. Opening the guide folder does not leave your current task in Companion.
After an update, close and reopen a guide that was already open so you see its new text.

21.2 Choose a guide format
Open the plain text copy in a text reader. Open the HTML copy in a browser to use its heading links.
Open the Word copy in Microsoft Word or another app that can read Word files. Open the EPUB copy in an ebook reader that supports that format.
The DAISY 3 copy contains text, not recorded speech. Unzip its files together, then open guide.opf in a compatible DAISY reader.
BRF is the braille-ready copy. It uses contracted Unified English Braille, with 40 cells per line and 25 lines per page.
The braille copy is made by software. It has not been checked by a human braille proofreader.
Each format has the same instructions. Release notes include the version in their file names. Older versioned notes can stay in the folder.
Updating the guides does not remove your personal files. The guides remain after you uninstall Companion.

22. FIX A PROBLEM

22.1 Companion hears the wrong words
Speak one short sentence or command at a time. Wait for the reply before speaking again.
Check the microphone choice and its position. Try a headset so the app is less likely to hear its own speech.
Use "Commands only" while reviewing text. Switch to "Normal mode" before writing by voice.
If a command still fails, press Alt+T, type it, and press Enter. Read the result before trying again.
Do not repeat a send, move, or delete command if the result was uncertain. Check what happened first.

22.2 You cannot hear speech
Check the computer's sound output and volume. Press Control to clear old speech, then give a new reading command.
Try "Use SAPI" if an AI voice is unavailable. AI speech needs an internet connection and a working service setup.
Use Voice options to check Speech volume and Speech rate. Ask your helper if no voice can be heard.

22.3 Email or a website will not work
For mail, check the selected account with "Which email account?". Ask your helper to check sign-in if needed.
For a page, try "Refresh page" or F5. Use "List links" if its layout is hard to explore.
If quick keys type letters, press Escape to leave text editing. Use the Companion page window for its reading keys.
A site may ask for human verification or use a control Companion cannot read. Ask for help rather than guessing.
Some email folder changes and forwarded attachments are limited by the service or this version. Review the related chapters before trying again.

22.4 A guide or update will not open
Close any open guide files, then say "Open user guides", or press Control+Shift+G, again. This asks Companion to refresh its guide copies.
If your Documents folder is not available, ask your helper to restore access to it.
For a failed update, keep using the current app if it opens. Try Check for updates again later.
Use the installed desktop icon. If it opens an older version, ask your helper to check the installation.
Tell your helper the app version, what you tried, and the exact error message. This makes the problem easier to find.

22.5 Understand privacy and online services
Offline speech works on your computer. It does not need to send your microphone audio to a speech service.
Online recognition needs your permission in Input options. An AI voice needs your permission in Synthesizer options. Having a service key does not grant permission on its own. Some older AI setups may need you to confirm permission again.
To stop online narration, choose another voice, turn off Allow narration text to be sent to OpenAI, and save. The app clears pending AI speech when you turn off permission.
The app keeps the documents, drafts, contacts, favorites, and history you use. Account tokens and AI keys are protected for your Windows user account. Documents and drafts remain local files, so protect your computer and Windows account.
Email, websites, audio streams, contacts sync, and update checks use the internet when you use those features. Service providers have their own data rules. The app does not include a user-tracking or advertising service.
The app skips history entries with private sign-in data. It also removes sign-in token values from older saved addresses. If a favorite needs sign-in again, open the site and save its main page.


23. REGULAR BROWSER CONNECTION

23.1 Set up Chrome and the extension
You need this only to use your own Chrome, with the sites you are already signed in to. Embedded Edge works without it.
1. Say "Set up Chrome". Or choose Set up Chrome in the Web browser menu, or in the web page's Alt menu. Or open Options, choose Web browser options, and choose Set up Chrome and the Voice Companion extension. Companion asks before each change. Say yes or no, or press Y or N.
2. If Chrome is missing, Companion offers to install it. This takes a few minutes. Companion tells you how it is going. If Windows asks for permission, press Alt+Y.
3. Companion then adds the extension and opens Chrome. Chrome asks you to turn it on. Press Tab until you hear "Enable extension", then press Enter. Companion reads the Chrome buttons to you while you do this.
4. If Chrome does not ask, press Alt+F to open the Chrome menu. Press Down Arrow until you hear "Voice Companion Browser Connection", then press Enter. Then choose Enable extension. If you are not in Chrome, press Alt+Tab.
5. When the extension links, you hear "Chrome is linked to Voice Companion." If it does not link in two minutes, Companion offers the Chrome Web Store page. There, press Tab until you hear "Add to Chrome", then press Enter. Chrome asks again. Press Tab until you hear "Add extension", then press Enter.
6. Say "Status" to hear the current step again. Say "Cancel" to stop setup.
7. When you open Web browser and Chrome is not ready, Companion asks: "Chrome is not set up yet. Set it up now?" This happens when Chrome is your guided browser but is not installed. It also happens when you open your regular browser and your Chrome is not linked. Press Y for yes or N for no, or say yes or no. Enter and Escape mean no. If you say no, Companion uses embedded Edge and does not ask again until you choose Chrome again.
8. The installer has a box named Set up Chrome and the Voice Companion extension when Voice Companion opens. It is off unless you tick it. When it is on, setup starts the next time Companion opens.
9. If setup does not finish, ask your helper. A helper can open Voice Companion Browser Connection in the Chrome Web Store and choose Add to Chrome. For Edge or a test copy, a helper can follow Browser Connection Setup in the Voice Companion Start menu folder.

23.2 Read and use your browser
1. Open Web browser from Companion's main menu. Choose Resume where you left off to read the active browser tab. Search, favorites, web history, Go back and Go forward remain in this menu.
2. Use your usual web keys and voice commands. Element keys, arrow reading, link and heading lists, table commands and forms use the same engine as the guided browser. See the Web browser chapters in this guide for each task.
3. Type and dictate in fields from Companion as usual. Tab moves among controls. Enter activates a control. Space selects a radio button or checkbox. Field editing, text selection, choices and form review keep their usual commands.
4. Escape returns to the Web browser menu as usual. From there, Escape returns to the main menu. You do not need to use a separate connection menu or switch to your browser to type.

23.3 Understand this testing connection
1. This uses your ordinary Chrome or Edge tab. The browser shows its normal notice while connected. Sites may still block access. PayPal sign-in has not been tested.
2. Companion links normal website tabs in this browser. New tabs and sites link on their own. When you switch tabs, Companion uses the active tab. Browser settings pages stay outside the link. It does not import your cookies.
3. If the link stops, keep Companion open. The extension tries again on its own. Companion does not open a separate guided browser in its place. Closing the connection leaves your regular browser tabs open.

Booking calendars
Choose a service before choosing a date. Some sites mark all dates as No information until you choose a service. Press Space or Enter on the radio button. Wait for the dates to load. Dates that the site disables stay marked unavailable.

24. FILE MANAGER

24.1 Open File manager
File manager is the first main menu choice. Say "File manager" or use Down Arrow and Enter. You start at the drive list. This list uses Windows Shell, the system behind File Explorer. It includes local drives, mapped drives, and cloud roots that Windows has registered. System folders such as Desktop and Documents are found inside drives. They are not drives. Cloud files may need a connection to download before they can be read.

24.2 Move through a list
Use Up Arrow and Down Arrow. Say "Next", "Previous", "Home", or "End". Home reaches the first item. End reaches the last. Lists do not wrap. You hear the item name, its type, and its place in the list. F5 or "Refresh" reads the folder again after changes made in another app.

24.3 Open a drive, folder, or file
Press Enter or say "Open", "Okay", or "That one" on the current item. You can also say its name. Say "C drive" to open that drive, or "Open" followed by a file name. A unique name can be used without its extension. If more than one name matches, use arrows or say the full path. Programs and unknown file types are not run.

24.4 Go to the parent folder
Escape goes up one folder. Say "Go back" or "Parent folder" for the same action. From Documents, then Voice Companion, Escape returns to Documents. At the top of a drive, Escape returns to the drive list. At the drive list, Escape returns to the main menu. Control+D or "Drive list" returns to drives. Say "Main menu" to leave File manager.

24.5 Select several items
Space checks or unchecks the current file or folder. Move to another item and press Space to add it. Moving does not clear the marked items. You can say "Select", "Unselect", "Mark", "Unmark", "Check", or "Uncheck". Add an item name to act on it directly. For example, say "Select notes.txt". Say "Select all" or press Control+A to mark all files and folders in this list. Say "Clear selection" or press Control+Shift+A to clear marks. Drives cannot be selected for file actions.

24.6 Copy and paste
Control+C or "Copy" copies the marked items. If nothing is marked, it uses the current item. Open the folder where you want the copies. Control+V or "Paste" puts them there. The clipboard is shared with File Explorer. You can copy in one app and paste in the other. If a name already exists, the action stops and tells you. It does not replace that file.

24.7 Cut and move
Control+X or "Cut" marks the items to move. Open the target folder, then press Control+V or say "Paste". Cut does not remove the files until paste succeeds. A folder cannot be placed inside itself. Windows checks access to the source and target folders.

24.8 Rename one item
Select just one item, or clear marks and move to it. Press F2 or say "Rename". Type or say the new name, with its extension, then press Enter. You can also say "Rename to" followed by the new name. Escape or "Cancel" leaves the name unchanged. A name that already exists is not replaced.

24.9 Delete files and folders
Delete or "Delete" asks you to confirm the named items. No is the first choice. Use Up Arrow to choose Yes and Enter to confirm. You can also say "Yes" or "No". Escape cancels. Windows handles recycling. If Windows cannot finish the action, check the folder before you try again. File manager does not provide a permanent-delete command. Drives cannot be deleted here.

24.10 Find commands and keys
Press Alt or say "File manager menu". Use arrows and Enter to choose an action. Each menu item reads its keyboard shortcut and voice command. Escape closes the menu. F1 or "File manager help" repeats the main commands. Press Alt+T to type a command when needed. While a slow location is loading, Escape cancels that opening request. File changes already in progress must finish; do not repeat the action until you hear its result.

24.11 Find and sort items
Type the first letter of a file, folder, or drive to select it. Repeat a letter to cycle matches. Type more letters within a short pause to narrow the list. Backspace removes a letter. Escape clears this name filter before moving up a folder. Control+E opens name search from any File Manager control. Control+F also searches names in this list. Control+Shift+F searches names in this folder and its subfolders. Control+Shift+X clears search. Say Search files for followed by a name, Search subfolders for followed by a name, or Clear search.
Alt+S opens Sort. Alt+N sorts by name, Alt+C by date created, Alt+M by date modified, and Alt+D by date added. Alt+A chooses ascending and Alt+Z descending. The older Control+Shift number and arrow keys also work. Say Sort by name, Sort by date created, Sort by date modified, Sort by date added, Sort ascending, or Sort descending for the same actions. Date added means first seen by this app in this folder. Dates that Windows does not supply sort last. Folders stay before files.
Tab and Shift+Tab cycle the list and the Search, Search subfolders, Sort, Clear search, Drives, and Main menu buttons. Enter activates a button. Alt+Home returns to the main menu. A subfolder search stops at 5,000 matches or 100,000 checked items. Narrow the search if you reach the limit. Links and junctions are not followed. Escape cancels a search.

25. DOCUMENT READER

25.1 Open supported files
Enter on a supported file opens it inside Voice Companion. The reader supports Word DOCX, TXT, MD, CSV, TSV, HTML, PDF, ODT, and EPUB. It does not need Microsoft Word. Files open read-only. Your source file is not changed. Choose Open a document in Word processor to edit a supported DOCX file. Word files with features the editor cannot keep open in the reader. Other supported files open for reading. Legacy DOC files and other unsupported types are not launched in another app.

25.2 Read with keys or speech
Use arrows to read by character or line. Control+Left and Control+Right move by word. You can select and copy text with the usual text keys. Voice choices include "Continue reading", "Next word", "Previous line", "Next sentence", and "Next paragraph".
To read the whole document, say "Read document", or press Control+Shift+R.
To reach an end, say "Go to beginning of document" or "Go to end of document", or press Control+Home or Control+End.
To move to a match, say "Find" followed by the text. Or press Control+F, type the text, and press Enter.
To move by heading, table, link, or list, say "Next heading" or "Next table", or press H, T, K, or L. Hold Shift to move back. For reader help, say "Help with document reader", or press F1.
Press Alt to open the document reader menu. It has Read, Move by, and Leave and help.

25.3 Return to your files
Escape or "Back to files" closes the reader view and returns to the same folder and item. "Main menu" returns to the app menu. You can use Options while reading. Saving Options names the reader when it returns you there.

25.4 Know the reading limits
HTML and book text are read without scripts or remote content. PDF pages have page labels. The reader uses offline AI OCR on image pages with no text. The models ship with the app; no page is sent to a web service. OCR can make errors. Low-score lines are reported. Check key facts in the source scan. Files are limited to 50 MB and five million text characters. Password-protected PDFs need to be unlocked in another tool first. Cloud files need their provider to make them available. Some layouts and graphics do not have text that can be read.

26. MEDIA PLAYER

26.1 Open and play a file
Choose Media from the main menu, then Media player. Radio and Podcasts are also in Media. Control+O opens the drive list. Use arrows and Enter to open a drive, folder, then a sound or video file. Escape goes up one folder. Escape at drives returns to the player. You can also press Enter on a sound or video file in File manager. Companion says the file name and starts to play it.

26.2 Control playback
Space plays or pauses. Control+S stops. Left and Right move back or forward 15 seconds. You hear how far you moved and where you are now, such as 1 minute 23 seconds of 4 minutes 5 seconds. At the start or end of the file, you hear that instead. Up and Down change volume. Control+I reads the file and playback state. Say Play, Pause, Stop media, Back 15 seconds, Forward 15 seconds, Volume up, or Volume down for the same actions. To move a different amount, say Back or Forward, followed by the time. For example, say Forward 2 minutes. To change speed, say "Playback speed", or choose it in the player menu. Then enter a number from 0.5 to 3 and press Enter.

26.3 Use the player menu
While a file plays, press Escape or Alt to open the player menu. You can also say Player menu. Use Up Arrow and Down Arrow to choose an item, then press Enter. Left and Right Arrow also move through the menu. Space, Control+S, and Control+I still work. Alt closes the menu and goes back to the player. Escape in the menu leaves the player. If you opened the file in File manager, you go back to the same folder and file. If not, you go back to Media. Audio stops unless Keep audio playing in the background is on. When you choose Media player from the Media menu, the menu is already open. For player help, say "Help with media player", or press F1.
