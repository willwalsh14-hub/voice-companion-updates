About this guide

Guide version: 0.2.182-test.

Voice Companion is a talking app for Windows. It speaks its own menus and messages. You can use your voice, your keyboard, or both. You do not need Microsoft Word or a screen reader to use it.

This guide has numbered chapters. Each chapter has short numbered parts. Each part starts with the task, then tells you what to say or which key to press.

Spoken commands are in quotation marks, such as "Help". Keys are written the way they are labeled, such as Control+F1 or Down Arrow. A plus sign means hold the first key, then press the next one.

New to Voice Companion? Read chapter 1 first. It covers the keys that work on every screen.

This is a test version. Some websites, email services, and audio streams may not work as expected. Listen to each message before you send, delete, or change anything important.

1. Getting Started

1.1 Install Voice Companion

The installer is the file named VoiceCompanion-Setup.exe. It puts the app and its guides on your computer.

  1. Open the installer.
  2. Follow its prompts.
  3. On the last page, leave Open Voice Companion now checked if you want to start the app.
  4. Choose Finish.

Later, open the app from its desktop icon or the Windows Start menu. Use the installed app, not an older copy from a download folder.

1.2 Open the app and wake it

  1. Open Voice Companion.
  2. Wait while it checks for updates.
  3. Listen to the ready message. It says the version, any news about an update, whether the microphone is on, and where you are.
  4. If it offers an update, say "Yes" or "No", or press Y or N.
  5. When it says it is ready, say "Wake up", or press Down Arrow. If the microphone is off, you start on the main menu, so use the arrow keys.
  6. Wait for the main menu. Then say the name of the tool you want.

For example, say "Word processor", "Notepad", "Email", "Web browser", "Radio", or "Podcasts".

The keyboard works even while voice commands are asleep. Press Down Arrow to hear a menu item. Press Enter to open it.

If a key press stops the ready message, say "What version?", or press Control+V. You hear the version and whether the microphone is on.

1.3 Use the main menu

The main menu is where every task starts. It has these choices, in this order:

  • File manager
  • Word processor
  • Document reader
  • Notepad
  • Email
  • Contacts
  • Calendar
  • Web browser
  • Media
  • Options
  • Check for updates
  • Help
  • Shut down the computer
  • Restart the computer
  • Exit Voice Companion

To move, say "Next" or "Previous", or press Down Arrow or Up Arrow.

To open the item you hear, say "That one", "Okay", or "Confirm", or press Enter.

To reach the first or last item, say "Start of list" or "End of list", or press Home or End.

The list does not wrap around. At the first or last item, you hear "Start of list" or "End of list".

To jump to an item, say its name, or press its first letter. Then press Enter to open it.

Keys that only work on the main menu:

  • To hear the version and whether the microphone is on, say "What version?", or press Control+V.
  • To hear the speech mode, say "What mode?", or press Control+M.
  • To open the guide folder, say "Open user guides", or press Control+G.
  • To start the lessons, say "Start tutorial", or press Control+T.
  • To open Help, say "Help", or press F1.

If the main menu does not know your words, it says "I did not understand that. You are on the main menu. Press F1 for help."

On the main menu, Alt says that you are in the main menu. The main menu is already a menu, so it has no Alt menu of its own.

On most other screens, press Alt to open a menu of that screen's actions. Each item says its key and its voice command. Use the arrows and Enter to choose. Press a number to open a group or choose an item. Type a letter to find an item. Escape goes back one level. Press Alt again to close the menu.

If a screen has no Alt menu, or you must answer a question first, Alt tells you why.

1.4 Keys that work everywhere

These keys work on every screen in Companion. Learn these first.

  • To go back to the main menu, say "Main menu", or press Control+F1. If a document has changes, Companion asks you to save it first.
  • To go back one step, press Escape.
  • To hear help for the screen you are on, press F1.
  • To open the menu for the screen you are on, press Alt.
  • To stop speech at once, press Control.
  • To type a command instead of saying it, press Alt+T.
  • To open Options, say "Options", or press Alt+O.
  • To pause or resume radio or a podcast, say "Pause audio", or press Control+P.
  • To hear what is playing, say "What's playing?", or press F3.
  • To hear the time, say "What time is it?", or press F12. Press F12 twice quickly for the date.
  • To pause voice commands, say "Go to sleep", or press Control+F12. Press Control+F12 again to wake up.
  • To turn the microphone fully off or on, press F9.
  • To make the text on the screen bigger or smaller, press Control+Plus or Control+Minus. See chapter 30.
  • To close Companion, say "Exit Companion", or press Alt+F4.

Keys for lists and menus:

  • Up Arrow and Down Arrow move through the items.
  • Home and End reach the first and last items.
  • Enter opens or chooses the item you hear.
  • A first letter jumps to a matching item.

Text boxes use letter keys for typing instead.

1.5 Set up your microphone

Voice Companion uses the microphone chosen in Windows. A built-in microphone works. A headset works better.

A headset keeps the app's own speech out of the microphone. It can also make your voice easier to hear.

If voice commands do not work, follow these steps.

  1. Open Windows sound settings and check the microphone.
  2. Check that apps are allowed to use it.
  3. Wait a moment. Companion keeps trying the microphone, and voice commands start by themselves when it works.
  4. If they still do not start, close Companion, then open it again.

You can keep using the keyboard if the microphone is not working.

2. Using Voice Commands

2.1 Give a command

Say a command, then wait for the reply. For example, say "Help", or press F1, to open the guide.

In a menu, say "Next", then "That one" to choose the item you hear. Or press Down Arrow, then Enter.

To return to the start, say "Main menu", or press Control+F1.

This guide gives each task as a spoken command, then a key. For example: say "Help", or press F1. The key does the same thing as the spoken command.

Names in the examples are sample names. Use your own document, contact, folder, or favorite name instead.

2.2 Speak text or give commands

Companion has three input modes. The mode decides what happens to your speech.

  • Normal mode: you can give commands and dictate text. In a document, draft, note, or web text box, ordinary speech adds text.
  • Commands only: speech is never added as text. Use it to move around without typing by mistake.
  • Dictation only: your speech becomes text. Some words that are usually commands become text too.

To change mode, say "Normal mode", "Commands only", or "Dictation only".

To choose a mode with the keyboard, press Alt+O, then choose Input options.

To hear the current mode, say "What mode?", or press Control+M on the main menu.

In Dictation only, you can still leave a document, cancel a draft, or put Companion to sleep. Some editing commands also still work.

If you are not sure which mode is on, switch to Normal mode before you give an editing or reading command.

2.3 Enter words that sound like commands

To write words that sound like a command, say "Type literally", followed by the words. For example, "Type literally new line" writes the words new line.

Without that start, "New line" makes a line break. "New paragraph" starts a new paragraph.

Say punctuation names as you speak. For example, "Hello comma Sam period" writes Hello, Sam.

Listen to the result. Speech recognition can pick the wrong word or mark.

Typed words are always text. Typing the word period does not add a period mark.

2.4 Type a voice command

Use this when you prefer the keyboard, or when a spoken command is not heard right.

  1. Press Alt+T to reach the command box.
  2. Type the command. For example, type list documents.
  3. Press Enter.

On a web page, Alt+T lets you type an app command. Press Escape to return to page reading.

2.5 Answer Yes or No

When Companion asks a question, listen to the item name and the action first.

  • To do the action, say "Yes", or press Alt+Y.
  • To stop it, say "No" or "Cancel", or press Alt+N or Escape.
  • You can also use the arrows or Tab to choose Yes or No, then press Enter.
  • Home selects Yes. End selects No.

2.6 Check the time and date

  • To hear the time, say "What time is it?", or press F12.
  • To hear the day and date, say "What's the date?", or press F12 twice quickly.
  • You hear the time in words, such as two forty-six PM or three oh seven AM.
  • Insert+F12 does the same thing as F12. Press it once for the time, or twice for the date.
  • You can also say "Time", "What's the time?", "Date", "Today's date", "What date is it?", or "What day is it?".
  • F12 works on every screen, in menus, and while the microphone is asleep. Your place does not change.
  • In a document, F12 is Save As. To hear the time there, press Insert+F12 or Caps Lock+F12, or ask "What time is it?"
  • In a document, email, or note, the single word "Time" or "Date" is written as text. Ask a full question there, such as "What time is it?"
  • A screen reader may answer Insert+F12 itself. F12 alone always reaches Companion, except in a document.

3. Using Help And Lessons

3.1 Open a Help topic

Help reads this guide to you inside the app. Each chapter is a Help topic.

To open Help, say "Help", or press F1 on the main menu. To choose a topic, say "Next", "Previous", and "That one", or use the arrows and Enter.

To go straight to a topic, say "Help with", followed by its name. For example, say "Help with documents", "Help with email", or "Help with web".

Other useful requests are "Help with radio", "Help with podcasts", "Help with presets", and "Help with commands".

On most screens, F1 opens the Help topic for that screen. Home and End reach the ends of a list.

If Help does not know your words, it says so, and says what to say next. While Help reads aloud, it does not answer, so talk in the room does not stop the reading.

Press Alt to open the help menu. It has Help topics, Subsections, Release notes, Lessons, the guide folder, Close help, and Main menu.

3.2 Read part of a topic

Each Help topic has a list of smaller parts. Choose the part you want, then listen.

Help reads one whole paragraph or list item at a time. Long paragraphs are read in full.

  • For the next block, say "Continue reading", or press Down Arrow.
  • To start the part again, say "Read from top".
  • To move by paragraph, say "Next paragraph" or "Previous paragraph", or press Down Arrow or Up Arrow.
  • To move by sentence, say "Next sentence" or "Previous sentence", or press Control+Down Arrow or Control+Up Arrow.
  • To move by word, say "Next word" or "Previous word", or press Control+Right Arrow or Control+Left Arrow.
  • For the first or last block, say "First block" or "Last block", or press Home or End.
  • To silence speech, press Control.

When you choose another Help item, the old speech stops first.

To return to the smaller parts, say "Back to subsections", or press Escape.

For the full topic list, say "Back to topics", or press Escape again. To return to your work, say "Close help", or press Escape from the topic list.

To hear the chapter and part you are in, say "Where am I?".

3.3 Start the audio tutorial

The tutorial is a set of spoken lessons. It waits for you between lessons. It explains the commands you give, but it does not carry them out. It never sends mail or changes your files.

  1. Return to the main menu.
  2. Say "Start tutorial", or press Control+T on the main menu.
  3. Say a course name, such as "Email" or "Web browser". You can also use the arrows and Enter.
  4. Listen to the first lesson.
  5. When you are ready, say "Next lesson", or press Down Arrow.

Courses cover Getting started, Word processor, Notepad, Email, Contacts and calendar, Web browser, Radio, Podcasts, YouTube, Options, Help and updates, and File manager.

3.4 Find, repeat, and leave a lesson

  • To hear the same lesson, say "Repeat", or press Control+R.
  • To go back a lesson, say "Previous lesson", or press Up Arrow.
  • For the lesson names, say "Lesson list", or press Control+L. Use the arrows and Enter to choose one.
  • To find a lesson, say "Find lesson" followed by a topic, such as "Find lesson favorites". Or press Control+F, type the topic, and press Enter.
  • For the course's command list, say "List commands", or press F1.
  • To learn about a command, say "Explain command", followed by the command, such as "Explain command send it". Or press Control+E, type the command, and press Enter.
  • If a long explanation pauses, say "Continue reading", or press Control+Down Arrow.
  • To go back one level, say "Go back", or press Escape. Say "Courses" to return to the course list.
  • To leave the tutorial, say "Main menu" or "Close tutorial", or press Escape from the course list.

Press Alt to open the lessons menu. It has the lesson list, Repeat, Find lesson, Explain a command, and the other lesson commands.

4. Speech And Voices

4.1 Stop or pause speech

To silence Companion, press Control. This works while the microphone is asleep too.

Control stops speech. It does not pause it. To hear the text again, use a reading key, such as Down Arrow.

By voice, say "Be quiet", "Stop talking", or "Shut up". Say "Resume" or "Keep talking" to go on with paused speech.

A new command stops the old speech and does the new task. For example, say "Next" or "Previous" to move while a menu item is read. Or say "Next message" while mail is read.

For radio or podcasts, say "Pause audio" or "Stop audio" to make clear you mean the sound. Or press Control+P to pause or resume.

4.2 Change speed, volume, or pitch

  • Say "Faster" or "Slower" to change the speech speed.
  • Say "Louder", "Quieter", or "Softer" to change the volume.
  • Say "Higher pitch" or "Lower pitch" to change the pitch one step at a time. Pitch is how high or low the voice sounds.

To set an exact value, say the setting name and a number:

  • Speed: say "Rate" or "Speed", then a number from minus ten to ten, such as "Rate four". Zero is normal speed.
  • Volume: say "Volume", then a number from zero to one hundred, such as "Volume fifty".
  • Pitch: say "Pitch", then a number from minus ten to ten, such as "Pitch minus three".

These settings change only Companion's voice. They do not change text size or music volume.

To use the keyboard, press Alt+O and open Voice options. Use Tab to reach a setting, then use the arrows to change it. Choose OK to save.

4.3 Choose a voice

  1. Say "Voice list" or "List voices".
  2. Choose the speech system you want. The list calls this a synthesizer.
  3. Choose a voice from that system.
  4. Listen to the sample and confirm your choice.

Use "Next voice", "Previous voice", and "Okay" to choose. Say "Cancel voice" to keep your old choice.

With the keyboard, press Alt+O and choose Synthesizer options. Tab to the voice list, choose with the arrows, then choose OK.

The speech systems are:

  • SAPI: the voices that come with Windows.
  • eSpeak: a small voice with US and UK English choices.
  • AI voice: an online voice. It needs a separate setup and an internet connection.

The voices you can choose depend on what is set up on your computer.

4.4 Use an AI voice

An AI voice reads some app text through an online service. A helper must set it up first.

  1. Open Options, then Synthesizer options.
  2. Your helper types the service key in the private AI API key box. This key is a code that lets the app use the speech service.
  3. Check the box Allow narration text to be sent to OpenAI. Check it only if you agree to send the text.
  4. Choose OK to save.
  5. Say "Use AI voice".

To go back to a local voice, say "Use SAPI" or "Use eSpeak".

An AI voice needs internet access and may use paid service credit. Long reading may take a moment to start.

Companion uses a local voice for typing and menu moves, so you hear each key at once. If online speech fails, Companion uses local speech.

5. Reading And Editing Text

5.1 Read from your current place

These commands work in documents, email, web pages, and notes.

  • To start at the top, say "Read from top" or "Start reading".
  • In a note or email draft, you can also press Control+R. In a document, press Control+Shift+R, because Control+R aligns text to the right there.
  • In the Document reader, say "Read document", or press Control+R.
  • For the next part, say "Continue reading", or press Down Arrow.
  • To move by letter or symbol, say "Next character" or "Previous character", or press Right Arrow or Left Arrow.
  • To move by word, say "Next word" or "Previous word", or press Control+Right Arrow or Control+Left Arrow.
  • To move by line, say "Next line" or "Previous line", or press Down Arrow or Up Arrow.
  • To move by sentence, say "Next sentence" or "Previous sentence". In Help, press Control+Down Arrow or Control+Up Arrow. In a document, press Alt, 5, then Q or R.
  • To move by paragraph, say "Next paragraph" or "Previous paragraph", or press Control+Down Arrow or Control+Up Arrow. In Help, press Down Arrow or Up Arrow.
  • To reach the start or end of a line, say "Go to start of line" or "Go to end of line", or press Home or End.

A line ends at a line break in the text. It may not match a line shown on the screen.

5.2 Read with the keyboard

These keys work in documents, email, and notes:

  • Left Arrow and Right Arrow move by character.
  • Control+Left Arrow and Control+Right Arrow move by word.
  • Up Arrow and Down Arrow move by line.
  • Control+Up Arrow and Control+Down Arrow move by paragraph.
  • Home and End reach the start or end of a line.
  • Control+Home and Control+End reach the start or end of the text.

In Help, Up Arrow reads the previous paragraph or list item. Down Arrow reads the next one. Home and End reach the first and last blocks of the topic.

On web pages, Page Up and Page Down move a longer way. Page reading starts from the place you last chose.

The same keys can review a received email. You can read and copy its text, but you cannot change that message.

5.3 Select text

Selected text is the part you have chosen to copy, change, or delete.

  • From the keyboard, hold Shift while you press a movement key.
  • To select all the text, say "Select all", or press Control+A.
  • For the current part, say "Select word", "Select line", "Select sentence", "Select character", or "Select paragraph".
  • To check what you chose, say "What is selected" or "Read selection". In a document, you can also press Insert+Shift+Down Arrow.

In a document or draft, say "Select", followed by the part you want. You can use first, next, previous, last, current, or a number with character, word, line, sentence, or paragraph. For example, say "Select first paragraph", "Select next paragraph", "Select previous paragraph", or "Select paragraph 3".

In an email address box, a subject box, a note, or a web text box, selection stays inside that box.

Web page text is read-only. To change a web answer, go into its text box first.

5.4 Choose named words

To select a word or phrase, say "Select", followed by the words. For example, say "Select red fox". In a document, you can also press Control+Alt+S, type the words, and press Enter.

To select a passage, say "Select", the start word, "through", and the end word. For example, say "Select cat through dog". You can use a phrase at either end, such as "Select red fox through sleeps".

You can also mark each end with its own command. Say "Selection start at", followed by the start word. Then say "Selection end at", followed by the end word. For example, say "Selection start at cat", then "Selection end at dog".

If the words appear more than once, Companion reads each place they match.

  • Say "Next" or "Previous" to hear another match. The arrows also move through the matches.
  • Say "Read sentence" to hear the nearby sentence again.
  • To choose a match, say "That one", "Confirm that", or "Okay", or press Enter.
  • To choose by number, say "Match", followed by its number, such as "Match 3". You can also say "First match" or "Second match".
  • To leave the text as it is, say "Cancel selection", or press Escape.

To select the word all, not all the text, say "Select the word all".

5.5 Copy, cut, paste, or remove text

  • Say "Copy selection", "Cut selection", or "Paste". Or press Control+C, Control+X, or Control+V.
  • In a document or draft, say "Copy", "Cut", or "Delete", followed by the words to change. For example, say "Copy cat", "Cut cat", or "Delete cat".
  • To remove the current part, say "Delete word", "Delete line", "Delete sentence", or "Delete paragraph". In a document, these are also in the Edit menu: press Alt, then 2.
  • To remove selected text, say "Scratch" or "Scratch that", or press Control+Shift+Z. With nothing selected, it removes the words you dictated last, where this works.
  • To reverse an edit in a document or draft message, say "Undo", or press Control+Z. Say it again to go back more steps. Companion says what it undid, such as "Undid bold".
  • To put back what you undid, say "Redo", or press Control+Y. A new edit clears what Redo can put back.
  • Typing from the keyboard is undone one sentence at a time. Companion keeps the last 100 changes. When there are no more, it says "Nothing more to undo".

When you move to a new place, the old selection is cleared. Check your choice again before you change or remove text.

5.6 Replace or insert words

To replace text, say "Replace", the old words, "with", and the new words. For example, say "Replace cat with dog". Or press Control+H, type the same words, and press Enter. You can also say "Change", the old words, "to", and the new words, such as "Change cat to dog".

If the app finds one match, it changes that text at once. If it finds several, use "Next", "Previous", and "That one" to choose the right place.

To stop without a change, say "Cancel replacement", or press Escape.

Say a punctuation name if you want it in the new text. For example, "Replace cat with dog period" puts a period after dog. A period that speech recognition adds by itself is not used.

To replace the current word, sentence, or paragraph, say "Replace word with", "Replace sentence with", or "Replace paragraph with", followed by the new text. For example, say "Replace word with hello", "Replace sentence with Thank you", or "Replace paragraph with I will call tomorrow".

To add text at a place, say "Insert before" or "Insert after", followed by words already in the text. For example, say "Insert before red fox". In a document, you can also press Alt, 2, then O or P.

Choose a matching place if asked. Then speak the words to add. In Commands only, say "Type literally" before the new words.

6. File Manager

6.1 Open File manager

File manager is the first choice on the main menu. Say "File manager", or press Enter on it. You start at the drive list. To start somewhere else, such as C drive or Documents, see File manager options in chapter 25.

The drive list comes from Windows, the same as in File Explorer. It has your local drives, network drives, and cloud folders such as OneDrive.

Desktop and Documents are folders inside a drive. They are not drives.

A cloud file may need an internet connection to download before you can read it.

6.2 Move through a list

  • To move, press Up Arrow or Down Arrow, or say "Next" or "Previous".
  • To reach the first or last item, press Home or End, or say "Home" or "End".
  • Lists do not wrap around.
  • You hear each item's name, its type, and its place in the list.
  • To read the folder again after a change in another app, say "Refresh", or press F5.

6.3 Open a drive, folder, or file

  • To open the item you hear, say "Open", "Okay", or "That one", or press Enter.
  • You can also say an item's name. For example, say "C drive" to open that drive.
  • To open a file by name, say "Open", followed by its name. If the name is unique, you can leave out the file type.
  • If more than one name matches, use the arrows, or say the full path.

Programs and unknown file types are never run from here.

6.4 Go to the parent folder

  • To go up one folder, say "Go back" or "Parent folder", or press Escape.
  • At the top of a drive, Escape goes to the drive list.
  • At the drive list, Escape goes to the main menu.
  • To go straight to the drive list, say "Drive list", or press Control+D.
  • To leave File manager, say "Main menu", or press Control+F1.

For example, from Documents, then Voice Companion, Escape goes back to Documents.

6.5 Select several items

  • To mark or unmark the current item, press Space.
  • To mark more items, move to each one and press Space. Moving does not clear the marks.
  • By voice, say "Select", "Unselect", "Mark", "Unmark", "Check", or "Uncheck". Add an item name to act on it directly, such as "Select notes.txt".
  • To mark every file and folder in the list, say "Select all", or press Control+A.
  • To clear the marks, say "Clear selection", or press Control+Shift+A.

Drives cannot be marked for file actions.

6.6 Copy and paste

  1. Mark the items. With no marks, Companion uses the current item.
  2. Say "Copy", or press Control+C.
  3. Open the folder where you want the copies.
  4. Say "Paste", or press Control+V.

The clipboard is shared with File Explorer. You can copy in one and paste in the other.

If a file with the same name is already there, nothing is replaced. Companion tells you.

6.7 Cut and move

  1. Mark the items.
  2. Say "Cut", or press Control+X.
  3. Open the folder where they should go.
  4. Say "Paste", or press Control+V.

Cut does not remove the files until the paste works. A folder cannot go inside itself. Windows checks that you may use both folders.

6.8 Rename one item

  1. Mark just one item, or clear the marks and move to it.
  2. Say "Rename", or press F2.
  3. Type or say the new name, with its file type, such as letter.txt.
  4. Press Enter.

You can also say "Rename to" followed by the new name. To keep the old name, say "Cancel", or press Escape. A name that is already used is not replaced.

6.9 Delete files and folders

  1. Mark the items, or move to one item.
  2. Say "Delete", or press Delete. Companion names the items and asks you to confirm.
  3. No is chosen first. To delete, press Up Arrow to choose Yes, then press Enter. Or say "Yes".

To keep the items, say "No", or press Escape.

Windows puts deleted items in the Recycle Bin when it can. File manager has no permanent delete. Drives cannot be deleted.

If Windows cannot finish, check the folder before you try again.

6.10 Find commands and keys

  • To open the File manager menu, say "File manager menu", or press Alt. Each item says its key and its voice command. Escape closes the menu.
  • To hear the main commands, say "File manager help", or press F1.
  • To type a command, press Alt+T.
  • If a slow location is loading, Escape stops that request.

A file change that has started must finish. Do not repeat it until you hear the result.

6.11 Find and sort items

To jump by name, type the first letter of a file, folder, or drive.

  • Type the same letter again to move to the next match.
  • Type more letters quickly to narrow the match.
  • Backspace removes a letter.
  • Escape clears the typed name before it moves up a folder.

To search by name:

  • To search this list, say "Search files for", followed by a name. Or press Control+E or Control+F.
  • To search this folder and its subfolders, say "Search subfolders for", followed by a name. Or press Control+Shift+F.
  • To clear the search, say "Clear search", or press Control+Shift+X.
  • Escape stops a search.
  • A subfolder search stops at 5,000 matches or 100,000 items. Narrow the search if you reach the limit. Links and junctions are not followed.

To sort, press Alt+S for the Sort menu, or use these keys:

  • To sort by name, say "Sort by name", or press Alt+N.
  • To sort by date created, say "Sort by date created", or press Alt+C.
  • To sort by date modified, say "Sort by date modified", or press Alt+M.
  • To sort by date added, say "Sort by date added", or press Alt+D. Date added means the date this app first saw the item here.
  • To sort from first to last, say "Sort ascending", or press Alt+A.
  • To sort from last to first, say "Sort descending", or press Alt+Z.

The older Control+Shift number keys and Control+Shift arrow keys also still work. Folders stay before files. Items with no date go last.

Tab and Shift+Tab move between the list and these buttons: Search, Search subfolders, Sort, Clear search, Drives, and Main menu. Enter uses a button. Alt+Home goes to the main menu.

6.12 Email a file

Choose a file in File manager. To send more than one, press Space on each file first. Folders cannot be sent.

  • To start a new email with the files attached, say "Email this file", or press Control+N. Then give the address, as in part 11.1.
  • To add the files to the email you are writing, say "Attach to draft", or press Control+O. You go back to the draft.

Both are also on the File manager menu. Press Alt to open it.

7. Document Reader

7.1 Open the Document reader menu

Document reader is on the main menu. Say "Document reader", or press Enter on it. Its menu has:

  • Open a document, Control+O. Browse to a file, then press Enter to read it.
  • Recent documents, Control+R. The files you read last, newest first.
  • Options, Alt+O. Choose whether a file is read aloud as soon as it opens.
  • Help, F1.
  • Main menu, Control+F1.

Escape in a document goes back to this menu. Escape on the menu goes to the main menu.

7.2 Open supported files

You can also press Enter on a file in File manager. It opens in the Document reader inside Voice Companion. You do not need Microsoft Word.

The reader opens these file types: Word DOCX, TXT, MD, CSV, TSV, HTML, PDF, ODT, and EPUB.

Files open read-only. Your file is never changed.

To edit a DOCX file, open it from the Word processor with Open a document. A Word file with features the editor cannot keep opens in the reader instead.

Old DOC files and other types are not opened in another app.

7.3 Read with keys or speech

  • To read the whole document, say "Read document", or press Control+R.
  • To read by character or line, use the arrow keys.
  • To move by word, press Control+Left Arrow or Control+Right Arrow.
  • Voice choices include "Continue reading", "Next word", "Previous line", "Next sentence", and "Next paragraph".
  • To reach the top or the end, say "Go to beginning of document" or "Go to end of document", or press Control+Home or Control+End.
  • To find text, say "Find" followed by the words. Or press Control+F, type the words, and press Enter.
  • To move by heading, table, link, or list, say "Next heading" or "Next table", or press H, T, K, or L. Hold Shift to move back.
  • To follow a link, press Enter on it.
  • To select and copy text, use the usual text keys.

For reader help, say "Help with document reader", or press F1.

Press Alt to open the document menu. It has Read, Move by, and Leave and help.

7.4 Return to your files

To close the reader, say "Back to files", or press Escape. You return to where you opened the file: the same folder and file in File manager, or the Document reader menu.

To go to the main menu, say "Main menu", or press Control+F1.

You can open Options while you read. When you save Options, you return to the reader.

7.5 Know the reading limits

  • Web pages and books are read without scripts or online content.
  • PDF pages have page labels.
  • A scanned PDF page with no text is read with offline text recognition, also called OCR. No page is sent to a web service.
  • OCR can make mistakes. Lines it is not sure of are reported. Check key facts against the paper copy.
  • Files can be up to 50 MB and five million characters.
  • A PDF with a password must be unlocked in another app first.
  • A cloud file must be downloaded by its service first.
  • Some layouts and pictures have no text to read.

8. Write A Document

8.1 Create and name a document

The Word processor makes files that Microsoft Word can open. You do not need Word installed.

Choose Word processor on the main menu to open its menu. It has these choices:

  • Create a document, or press Control+N. Companion asks for a name, then starts a blank file.
  • Open a document, or press Control+O. This starts at the File manager start place. That is the drive list unless you changed it.
  • Recent documents, or press Control+R. This lists files you used in the app.
  • Options, or press Alt+O. This opens Word processor options.

For help, say "Help with documents", or press F1 in the Word processor menu.

To start a document:

  1. Say "Create a document". Or choose Word processor, then press Control+N.
  2. Companion says, "Please say or type the name for your document, then press Enter or say confirm."
  3. Type the name, such as My Letter, and press Enter. Or say the name. Companion says it back. Then say "Confirm", or press Enter.
  4. Speak a short sentence or type into the document.
  5. To check what you wrote, say "Read paragraph", or press Alt, 5, then B.

To stop without making a document, press Escape or say "Cancel" while Companion asks for the name.

To change the name later, say "Name document", followed by the new name. Or press F2, type the name, and press Enter.

The file name and a title in your letter are separate. Naming the file does not add a title to the page.

8.2 Write and move through the document

  • To start a new paragraph, say "New paragraph", or press Control+Enter. Enter also makes a new paragraph when you type.
  • To start a new line in the same paragraph, say "New line".
  • To review, say "Read paragraph", "Spell word", "Next word", or "Next sentence". The reading keys are in chapter 5.
  • To move to the top or end, say "Go to beginning of document" or "Go to end of document", or press Control+Home or Control+End.
  • You can also say "Move to" or "Jump to" in place of "Go to", such as "Jump to end of document".

After you move, new words go in at that place. An old selection is cleared.

To give any document command with the keyboard, press Alt for the document menu. See part 8.9.

8.3 Change the look of text

Select the text first if you want to change only part of it. With nothing selected, many commands change the current paragraph.

  • For bold, italic, or underline, say "Make this bold", "Make this italic", or "Underline this". Or press Control+B, Control+I, or Control+U. Each key turns the style on or off.
  • To turn a style off, say "Remove bold", "Remove italic", or "Remove underline".
  • To format certain words, say "Bold", "Italicize", "Underline", or "Highlight", followed by those words. For example, say "Bold cat" or "Highlight cat".
  • To choose a font, say "Set font to", followed by its name, such as "Set font to Arial". Or press Control+Shift+F, type the name, and press Enter.
  • To choose a size, say "Set font size to", a number, and "points", such as "Set font size to 14 points". Or press Control+Shift+P, type the size, and press Enter.
  • To set both, say "Set font", the font name, the size, and "points". For example, say "Set font Times New Roman 16 points".

To change just the current word:

  • Say "Bold word", "Italic word", or "Underline word". "Remove bold from word" turns bold off.
  • To change its size, say "Set word size to", a number, and "points", such as "Set word size to 14 points".
  • To change its font, say "Set word font to", followed by the font name, such as "Set word font to Arial".

To hear the text settings, say "Say font" or "Read formatting", or press Insert+F. To hear one setting, say "Say font name", "Say size", "Say bold", "Say italic", or "Say underline".

If selected text has more than one setting, Companion says the setting is mixed.

8.4 Use headings and align text

A heading names a part of your document. Heading levels run from 1 to 6. Level 1 is the main heading. A higher number makes a smaller heading.

  • To make a heading, say "Heading", followed by a level number, such as "Heading 1". Or press Control+Alt and the number, such as Control+Alt+1.
  • "Make this a heading" uses level 1.
  • To remove the heading style, say "Normal text", or press Control+Shift+N.
  • To place a paragraph on the page, say "Align left", "Align center", "Align right", or "Align justified". Or press Control+L, Control+E, Control+R, or Control+J.
  • Justified text lines up with both page edges.
  • To check, say "Say alignment" or "Say heading".

With text selected, these commands change all the selected paragraphs.

8.5 Change line spacing

Line spacing is the space between the lines of a paragraph.

  • For single or double spacing, say "Single space" or "Double space", or press Control+1 or Control+2.
  • For one and a half, say "Line spacing 1.5", or press Control+5.
  • For another value, say "Line spacing", followed by the number. Or press Alt, 3, then I, and type the number.
  • For an exact value, say "Custom spacing". Give the value when asked, such as "One point seven five" or "Exactly eighteen points". Companion repeats it. Say "Yes" to use it, "No" to give another value, or "Cancel" to stop.
  • For the whole document, say "Set document line spacing to", followed by the value, such as "Set document line spacing to 1.25".
  • To check, say "Read line spacing" for this paragraph, or "Read document line spacing" for the whole document.
  • To undo spacing changes, say "Reset line spacing" for this paragraph, or "Reset document line spacing" for all paragraphs.

These choices are also in the Format menu: press Alt, then 3. A new paragraph keeps the spacing of the one before it.

8.6 Make a list

A bulleted list marks each item with a dot. A numbered list gives each item a number.

  1. Say "Bulleted list" or "Numbered list", or press Control+Shift+L or Control+Shift+7.
  2. Speak or type the first item.
  3. For the next item, say "New paragraph", or press Enter.
  4. To go back to plain text, say "End list", or press Alt, 4, then F.

8.7 Make a table

A table is a grid of boxes in rows and columns. Each box is called a cell.

  1. Say "Create table with", the number of rows, "rows and", the number of columns, and "columns". For example, say "Create table with two rows and two columns". Or press Control+Alt+T and type the size.
  2. Speak the text for the first cell.
  3. To move to the next cell, say "Next cell", or press Tab.
  4. To check your work, say "Read table", or press Alt, 8, then A.

To move around a table:

  • "Previous cell", or press Shift+Tab.
  • "Next row" or "Previous row", or press Alt+Down Arrow or Alt+Up Arrow.
  • "Next column" or "Previous column", or press Alt+Right Arrow or Alt+Left Arrow.

To change a table:

  • "Read cell" reads the current cell. "Clear cell" removes its words.
  • "Add row" and "Add column" make the table bigger. "Delete row" and "Delete column" remove those parts.
  • "Leave table" moves you below the table to keep writing.
  • "Delete table" removes the whole table. Say "Undo" at once if that was a mistake.

All table commands are in the Table menu: press Alt, then 8.

The editor makes simple tables with straight rows and columns. It may not open more complex tables from other programs.

8.8 Save, copy, close, or reopen a document

Companion saves as you write.

  • To save under the current name, say "Save", "Save document", or press Control+S. A document with no name asks for a name first. "Save" in a sentence, such as "Save the date for Friday", is text.
  • To save a copy with a new name or in another folder, use Save As. See the steps below.
  • To rename the file, say "Name document", followed by the new name, such as "Name document My Letter". Say "Undo" right after a rename to get the old name back.

To close the document:

  1. Say "Leave document" or "Go back", or press Control+W or Escape.
  2. Companion asks, "Save this document?"
  3. Say "Yes" to keep the current name, or to name an untitled file. Say "No" to throw away the changes since you last opened, saved, or named the file. Press Escape to keep editing.

To save a copy with Save As:

  1. Say "Save as", or press F12. Control+Shift+S also works. It is also on the File menu: press Alt, then 1.
  2. Say or type the new name. Then press Enter or say "Confirm".
  3. Choose the folder. To use Documents, in the Voice Companion folder, press Enter or say "Confirm". Or say a folder, such as "Desktop" or "Downloads". You can also type a full folder path.
  4. For a file you opened from another folder, you can say "Same folder" to save the copy next to it.
  5. If a file with that name is already in the folder, Companion asks whether to replace it. Say "Yes" to replace it. Say "No" to choose another name.

After Save As, you are working in the new file. The first file stays as it was.

You can also say the name with the command, such as "Save as My Letter Copy". Then choose the folder.

New documents always go in Documents, in the Voice Companion folder. Saving or opening a file in another folder does not change that.

To return to the document while Companion is still open, say "Resume document".

To reopen a document later:

  • Say "List documents", then choose a file.
  • Or choose Word processor, then press Control+R for recent files, or Control+O to open one.
  • To open a file by name, say "Open document", followed by its saved name, such as "Open document My Letter".

Your files are kept in Documents, in the Voice Companion folder. They use the .docx file type. To open that folder in Windows, say "Open documents". To hear where it is, say "Where are my documents?".

Companion can reopen the simple files it makes. It may refuse a complex Word file, so that it does not change it the wrong way.

8.9 Document command menu

In a document, press Alt for the document menu. Its groups are File, Edit, Format, Insert, Read and navigate, Dictation, Table, and More commands.

Use the arrows and Enter, or press a group number. Type a letter to find an item. Escape goes back a level, then back to the document.

Each item works the same as its voice command. If an item needs a value, type or say it and press Enter. Escape cancels.

Document keys:

  • Control+S saves. F12 saves as. Control+Shift+S also saves as. F2 names the document.
  • Control+N creates a document. Control+O opens one. Control+W closes it after a save question.
  • Control+Z undoes. Control+Y redoes. Control+A selects all.
  • Control+C copies. Control+X cuts. Control+V pastes.
  • Control+F finds text. Control+H replaces text: type the old words, the word with, and the new words.
  • Control+B, Control+I, and Control+U turn bold, italic, and underline on or off.
  • Control+L, Control+E, Control+R, and Control+J align left, center, right, and justified.
  • Control+Shift+F chooses a font. Control+Shift+P chooses its size.
  • Control+1, Control+2, and Control+5 set single, double, and one and a half spacing.
  • Control+Enter makes a new paragraph.
  • Control+Shift+R reads the document.
  • F4 starts dictation. Shift+F4 pauses it.
  • F1 opens help. Alt+O opens Word processor options. Alt+T lets you type any voice command.
  • In a table, Tab and Shift+Tab move by cell. Alt+Up Arrow and Alt+Down Arrow move by row. Alt+Left Arrow and Alt+Right Arrow move by column.

10. Set Up Email

10.1 Connect an email account

Connect an account before you rely on Companion to send or sort email. Companion uses email in two ways:

  • Thunderbird, for Gmail, Outlook, Microsoft 365, and most other email.
  • Yahoo, for a Yahoo address.
  1. Say "Add account". Or press Alt+O, choose Email options, then choose Add an email account.
  2. Choose Thunderbird or Yahoo. Say its name, or press T or Y, then Enter.
  3. Follow the steps Companion gives. Ask your helper if you need help with a sign-in page.
  4. Open the inbox and send a test message to someone you know.

You can also say "Add Gmail account" or "Add Outlook account". Companion adds it to Thunderbird. See part 10.4.

Older versions of Companion could connect Gmail and Outlook by themselves. They now work only through Thunderbird. If you had one of those accounts, Companion removes it the first time it starts and tells you. Your drafts and contacts are kept. To use that address again, say "Add account" and choose Thunderbird.

Yahoo uses an app password, not your normal password. To make one, sign in to Yahoo, open Account security, and choose Generate app password. Copy it. Then follow these steps.

  1. Choose Yahoo. Companion asks for your email address.
  2. Type or say the address, then press Enter.
  3. Type or paste the app password, then press Enter. Press Control+V to paste. For privacy, do not say it aloud. You hear "Hidden character" for each letter.
  4. Companion checks the password with Yahoo. It tells you if it worked, or what to do if it did not.

To fix the address, say "Change address". To stop, say "Cancel", or press Escape. If Yahoo stops taking a saved app password, make a new one. Then say "Add account" and choose Yahoo again. The new password replaces the old one.

Thunderbird is a free email program. It keeps your sign-in, so Companion does not need its own. When you choose Thunderbird, Companion checks what is missing. It fixes each thing when you say yes. Say yes or no, or press Y or N.

  • If Thunderbird is not on the computer, Companion offers to install it. This takes a few minutes. If Windows asks whether to allow changes, press Alt+Y.
  • If Thunderbird has no email account yet, Companion helps you add one. See part 10.4.
  • You never need to open Thunderbird yourself. Companion starts it in the background and keeps it minimized. Your keys stay in Companion. If it takes more than a moment, you hear "Starting Thunderbird". Then your command goes on by itself. To also keep it off the taskbar, see Thunderbird window in part 25.5.
  • Thunderbird 154 and newer work better with Microsoft 365. If your Thunderbird is older, or is the yearly ESR kind, Companion offers to update it the first time you use email. Say yes, and Companion does the rest. It takes a few minutes. Your email accounts, passwords, and settings stay as they are.
  • To update Thunderbird at any time, say "Update Thunderbird". Companion first tells you which version you have. It never closes Thunderbird while you are writing a message there. After the update, Companion checks that each of your accounts is still there. If one is not, Companion puts your settings back and tells you. Nothing is lost.

Enter passwords and codes privately. Do not read them aloud to someone you do not trust.

If account setup is not available, ask your helper to check the app's email setup.

10.2 Choose or remove an account

  • To connect another address, say "Add another account".
  • To hear your saved accounts, say "List email accounts".
  • To hear the account in use, say "Which email account?". Check it before you send mail.

To switch to another account:

  1. Say "Switch account". Or in email, press Alt, 6, then 2.
  2. You hear each account, such as "Thunderbird, sam@gmail.com, 2 of 3". The account in use comes first and is called current.
  3. Press Up Arrow or Down Arrow, then Enter. Or say "Next" and "That one". You can also say the number, the service, or the address, such as "2", "Yahoo", "Gmail", or "sam at gmail dot com".

Companion switches to that account and opens its inbox. To keep the account you have, say "Cancel", or press Escape.

You can also say "Switch to", followed by the service name, such as "Switch to Yahoo" or "Switch to Gmail". Gmail and Outlook find the Thunderbird account with that kind of address. This works when only one such account is connected. Otherwise, say "Switch to" and the full address.

"Check Gmail", "Check Outlook", and "Check Yahoo" open that account when only one such account is connected.

With the keyboard, you can also press Alt+O and choose Email options. Sending account names the account in use. Choose another one there to switch.

Each account starts with its kind, Thunderbird or Yahoo, such as "Thunderbird, sam@gmail.com". You can connect a Yahoo address two ways, straight to Yahoo and through Thunderbird. Then you hear both, "Yahoo, sam@yahoo.com" and "Thunderbird, sam@yahoo.com". To pick one by voice, say the kind, then the address. For example, say "Switch to Thunderbird, sam at yahoo dot com".

To remove an account from Companion:

  1. Say "Remove email account". Or in email, press Alt, 6, then 4.
  2. Choose the account from the list, the same way as above.
  3. Listen to the question. It names the account.
  4. Say "Yes" to remove it, or "No" to keep it. Or press Y or N.

Nothing is removed until you say yes. Pressing Enter alone answers no.

Removing an account only removes its saved sign-in from Companion. Your email stays with your email service. Your other accounts do not change. If you remove the account in use, Companion picks the next one and tells you which.

To fully end access, a helper can also do this:

  • Yahoo: delete the app password in Yahoo Account security.
  • Thunderbird: nothing more. Removing it only stops Companion from using that account. Thunderbird does not change.

To connect the account again later, say "Add account".

Accounts are also in the email menu. In email, press Alt, then 6. Choose Add account, Switch account, Which account, Remove account, or Add Thunderbird account.

10.3 Add and find contacts

A contact is a saved name with an email address or a phone number. Contacts has its own screen on the main menu. See chapter 13.

  • To save a contact, say "Add contact", the person's name, "as", and their email address. For example, say "Add contact Bob as bob@example.com". Or in the inbox, press Alt, 5, then 3. Type the name, the word as, and the address, then press Enter.
  • To hear saved names, say "List contacts", or press P in the inbox. While you write an email, press Control+K. Use the arrows to hear each name. Press Enter to hear the address.
  • To find a contact, say "Find contact", followed by the saved name, such as "Find contact Bob".
  • When Companion asks who to email, you can say a saved contact's name.

Your Google and Outlook contacts come from Thunderbird. Contacts saved only on this computer are kept too. Contacts brought in from Google Contacts before are kept, and you can change them.

Your Google calendar is on the Calendar screen. See chapter 14. Google Drive is not connected in this version.

10.4 Add a Thunderbird account

You can add an email account to Thunderbird without leaving Companion. Each address becomes its own account in Companion. You can add several, one after another.

  1. Say "Add Thunderbird account". Or in email, press Alt, 6, then 5.
  2. Type or say the email address, then press Enter.
  3. The first time, Companion asks for the name people see on your email. Type or say it, then press Enter.
  4. Companion opens the Thunderbird page for adding an account. It stays in the background. Companion fills in your name and address, and reads the page to you.
  5. Say "Continue in Thunderbird" to choose the main button on that page. Companion reads the page again when it changes.
  6. Sign in when asked. The ways to sign in are below.
  7. Wait for Companion. It tells you when the account is ready. The new account is connected and selected.

How you sign in depends on the email service:

  • Gmail and Outlook: the sign-in page opens in the Companion web browser. Sign in there as on any web page. Thunderbird gets the answer by itself. This needs Thunderbird 153 or newer. With an older Thunderbird, Gmail and Outlook sign in like Yahoo, below.
  • To use the other browser for that page, say "Open sign-in page in Chrome" or "Open sign-in page in embedded Edge".
  • Microsoft 365 for work or school: Thunderbird can connect in more than one way. Companion chooses Microsoft Graph, as Mozilla suggests. You hear "It will connect with Microsoft Graph". This needs Thunderbird 154 or newer. Then Microsoft asks you to sign in, as for Outlook.
  • Most other email: Thunderbird asks for a password. Companion opens a private box. Type or paste the password, then press Enter. It goes straight to Thunderbird. Companion does not say it or save it.
  • Yahoo and AOL: Thunderbird shows their sign-in page in its own window. Companion cannot read that window. Say "Show Thunderbird" to bring it to the front. Then use a screen reader, or ask a helper. Press Alt+Tab to come back.

While Companion waits, you can say:

  • "What does Thunderbird say?" to hear the page again.
  • "Enter Thunderbird password" to open the private password box.
  • "Show Thunderbird" to bring Thunderbird to the front.
  • "Stop waiting for Thunderbird" to stop. Nothing is removed.

If Thunderbird already has the address, Companion connects it at once.

Companion waits up to 15 minutes. If you finish in Thunderbird later, say "Add account" and choose Thunderbird. Companion connects every new address it finds.

11. Write An Email

11.1 Start a new message

  1. Say "Write an email" from the main menu. In email, say "New email", or press N.
  2. Give the address or a saved contact name when asked.
  3. Give the subject when asked.
  4. Speak the message in short parts. Listen back before you send.

With the keyboard, type an address in the To box and press Enter. Then go on to the subject and the message.

To speak an email address, say "at" for @ and "dot" for each period. For example, say "sam at example dot com" for sam@example.com. Say "underscore" for _ and "dash" for -.

Press Alt to open the email draft menu. It has Message, Fields, Format, Contacts and help, Attachments, and Undo and redo. Message has Send, Save draft, Read draft, Save and close, and Discard draft. While the menu is open, the keys you press choose items. They are not added to the message.

11.2 Change To, CC, BCC, or Subject

A message has these parts:

  • To: the main person you are writing to.
  • CC: sends a copy to another person.
  • BCC: sends a copy without showing that address to the others.
  • Subject: what the message is about.
  • Body: the message itself.

To reach a part, say "Go to To", "Go to CC", "Go to BCC", "Go to Subject", or "Go to Body". Or press Tab and Shift+Tab.

Type the address or subject and press Enter. Left Arrow and Right Arrow review the letters. Backspace and Delete remove them.

  • To set the To box by voice, say "Email to", followed by the address, such as "Email to sam@example.com".
  • To set the subject, say "Subject is", followed by the subject, such as "Subject is Hello from me".
  • To add a copy address, say "CC" or "BCC". Say "Skip" if you do not want one.
  • To check a part, say "Read recipient", "Read subject", "Read CC", or "Read BCC".

11.3 Write and format the message

Ordinary speech adds text to the message. "New paragraph" starts a paragraph. "Read paragraph" reads it back.

The selection and formatting commands in chapters 5 and 8 work in the message body too.

  • For bold, italic, or underline, say "Toggle bold", "Toggle italic", or "Toggle underline", or press Control+B, Control+I, or Control+U.
  • You can also change a font, text size, line spacing, heading, list, or table.
  • The address and subject boxes use plain text only.
  • The person who reads your mail may see other fonts or spacing in their email app.
  • To write words that sound like commands, say "Type literally" first.
  • To reverse an edit, say "Undo", or press Control+Z. Undo also brings back an address or subject you changed. To put it back, say "Redo", or press Control+Y.
  • To hear the whole draft, say "Read email draft", or press Control+R.

For help, say "Help with writing email", or press F1.

11.4 Save or cancel a draft

  • To keep a message for later, say "Save draft" or "Save", or press Control+S.
  • To hear your saved drafts, say "List email drafts".
  • To reopen a saved draft, say "Open email draft", or press D in the inbox. Use the arrows to choose a draft, then press Enter.
  • To open a draft by name, say "Open email draft", followed by its name, such as "Open email draft Untitled".
  • To save the draft and close it, say "Leave email". You go back to your email, or to the main menu if email was not open.

To leave an unsent draft, say "Cancel", "Back", or "Back to inbox", or press Escape. This works in a new message, a reply, a reply to all, or a forward, from any part of the draft.

If you wrote something that is not saved, Companion asks first: save it as a draft, throw it away, or keep writing.

  • Save as a draft is chosen first. Press Enter, or say "Save draft", to keep it and go back to your email.
  • To throw the changes away, say "Throw it away", or press Down Arrow, then Enter.
  • To go back to the message, say "Keep writing" or "No", or press Escape. Nothing is lost.

A draft with nothing written closes without the question. Throwing a draft away cannot take back a message already sent. If you reopened a saved draft, throwing it away keeps its earlier saved copy.

11.5 Review and send

  1. Say "Send email", "Send it", or "Send that", or press Control+Enter or Alt+S in the draft.
  2. Listen to the sending account, addresses, subject, and message.
  3. If something is wrong, say "Cancel send", or press Alt+N. Fix it, then start the review again.
  4. Say "Yes", or press Alt+Y, only when the review is right.

Nothing is sent before the review and confirmation. If no account is connected, Companion saves a draft instead.

When the email service accepts the message, Companion says it was sent. Check delivery with your helper during early tests.

If Companion says the result is uncertain, check your sent mail and ask the person before you try again.

With Yahoo, Companion also puts a copy in your Sent folder. If it cannot, it tells you. The message was still sent.

The review also names the files attached. If a file was moved or deleted after you attached it, nothing is sent. Companion tells you which file, and the draft is kept.

11.6 Attach files

You can attach files to a new message, a reply, a reply to all, or a forward.

To attach a file by its name, say "Attach", the name, "from", and the folder. For example, say "Attach my resume from Documents".

  • The folder can be Documents, Desktop, Downloads, Pictures, Music, or Videos. Companion also looks in the folders inside it.
  • If one file fits, Companion attaches it. You hear, for example, "Attached resume.docx. 1 attachment."
  • If several files fit, you hear a short list, newest first. Use Up Arrow and Down Arrow, then press Enter on the file you want. Escape closes the list.
  • To attach the file you downloaded last, say "Attach the last download".

To choose the file in File manager:

  1. In the draft, say "Attach a file", or press Control+O. File manager opens.
  2. Move to the file. To attach several files from one folder, press Space on each one.
  3. Press Enter. Companion attaches the files and takes you back to the same place in the draft.

Folders cannot be attached. Escape in the File manager drive list goes back to the draft without attaching anything.

To check what is attached:

  • To hear what is attached, say "Attachments", "What's attached", or "List attachments", or press Control+L. The Attachments list opens. Part 12.7 tells how to use it.
  • To hear how big the files are, say "How big are the attachments".
  • To take a file off, say "Remove" and its name, such as "Remove report" or "Remove the photo". You can also say "Remove attachment report.pdf". Or press Delete in the Attachments list.

These commands work only as whole commands. A sentence such as "Remove the photo from the album" is written into your message.

Gmail and Yahoo take about 25 megabytes for each message. Email makes files about a third larger. Companion warns you when the files may be too large to send.

A saved draft keeps its attached files. When you open it again, they are still there.

12. Read And Organize Email

12.1 Read messages

  1. Say "Email" or "Check email", or choose Email on the main menu.
  2. Listen to the sender, subject, and date of the selected message.
  3. To hear another message, say "Next message" or "Previous message", or press Down Arrow or Up Arrow.
  4. To read it, say "That one" or "Open current message", or press Enter. Companion says "Message opened." Then it reads the message.

To open a message by number, say "Read message" or "Open message", followed by its number, such as "Read message 1" or "Open message 1".

To repeat the sender, subject, and date, say "Message details", or press I. To hear one part of the header, use these keys or questions. They work in the message list and in an open message:

  • Alt+1: who it is from. Say "Who is it from?" or "Who sent this?"
  • Alt+2: who it is to. Say "Who is it to?"
  • Alt+3: the CC line. Say "Read the CC." If nobody got a copy, you hear "No CC."
  • Alt+4: the subject. Say "What is the subject?"
  • Alt+5: the date it was sent. Say "When was it sent?" or "Read the date."
  • Alt+6: how many files are attached. Say "How many attachments?"

For more of a long message, say "Continue reading", or press Down Arrow.

To read it again from the top, say "Read from beginning". To go back to the message list, say "Back to folder" or "Go back", or press Escape.

Press Alt to open the email menu. Its groups are Messages, Select, Go to folder, Folder actions, Drafts and contacts, Accounts, Pages, Options and help, and Header and attachments. For example, press Alt, 3, then 2 to go to Sent. Press Alt, 8, then 1 for Email options.

The single-letter email keys are listed in part 12.6.

12.2 Go to folders

To choose a folder from a list, say "Go to folder", or press G. Use "Next", "Previous", and "That one", or the arrows and Enter.

With more than one email account, you hear your accounts first. The account in use comes first. Choose an account, or say its number, service, or address. Then you hear that account's folders.

Escape in the folder list goes back to the account list. Escape in the account list closes it, and you stay where you were.

To open a folder by name, say "Go to folder", "Open folder", or "Go to", followed by its name. For example, say "Go to folder Receipts", "Open folder Receipts", or "Go to Receipts". This always uses the account you are in.

  • To go back to the Inbox, say "Back to inbox", or press G and choose Inbox.
  • To hear where you are, say "Which folder", or press W.
  • For a folder inside another folder, give its full place, such as "Go to Projects slash 2026" or "2026 inside of Projects".
  • If two folders have the same name, listen to each full place. Choose the right one with "Next" and "That one".
  • In any account, say "Go to Spam", "Go to Junk", "Go to Trash", "Go to Sent", or "Go to Drafts". In Yahoo, Spam is the folder Yahoo calls Bulk.
  • Escape cancels the folder list.

12.3 Select, move, or delete messages

  • To mark the message you hear, say "Mark", "Select", or "Mark message", or press Space. Press Space again to remove the mark.
  • To mark more, move to another message and mark it too.
  • To extend the marks, say "Extend selection next" or "Extend selection previous", or press Shift+Down Arrow or Shift+Up Arrow.
  • To mark every message on this page, say "Select all messages", or press Control+A.
  • To hear how many are marked, say "What is selected", or press S.
  • To remove all marks, say "Clear selection", or press U.
  • To move marked messages to Trash, say "Delete", or press Delete. With no marks, it acts on the current message.
  • In Trash, Delete changes nothing. Companion says the message is already there.
  • To pick a folder to move them to, say "Move", or press M. To name the folder, say "Move to", followed by its name, such as "Move to Saved".

"Delete message" and "Move message" act on the current message, even if other messages are marked.

Listen to the action and the names, then answer Yes or No. If a result is uncertain, check the folder before you try again.

Inside an open message, Control+A selects text to copy. It does not mark other messages.

12.4 Reply or forward

Open the message first. Then say "Reply", "Reply all", or "Forward". Or press R, A, or F.

Reply all goes to the sender and everyone else on the message. Check the addresses before you send.

For a forward, give the new address when asked. Speak your added text. Then review and send as in part 11.5.

A forward includes the original attachments. Companion says their names when the forward starts. To leave one out, say "Remove" and its name, such as "Remove the photo". Or open the Attachments list with Control+L, move to the file, and press Delete.

To leave the reply, say "Cancel" or "Back to inbox". If you wrote something, Companion asks whether to save it as a draft, throw it away, or keep writing. Then you are back at the message you were reading.

12.5 Create and manage folders

  • To create a folder, say "Create folder", followed by the name, such as "Create folder Receipts". Or press Alt, 4, then 1.
  • To create a folder inside another, say "Create folder", the name, "inside", and the other folder's name. For example, say "Create folder 2026 inside Receipts". Leave out the last name to choose it from a list, as in "Create folder 2026 inside".
  • To rename a folder, say "Rename folder", or press F2, and choose it. Then say "Rename to", followed by the new name, such as "Rename to Bills". Or give both names at once, such as "Rename folder Receipts to Bills".
  • To move a folder, say "Move folder", or press Alt, 4, then 3. Or give both names at once, such as "Move folder Bills into Archive".
  • To delete a folder, say "Delete folder", or press Alt, 4, then 4, and choose it. Or name it, such as "Delete folder Bills".

Listen to the full folder place and the action. Say "Yes" to go ahead or "No" to cancel.

You can delete only empty folders you made. Built-in folders such as Inbox are protected.

Some email services limit folder changes. For example, Yahoo does not let you rename, move, or delete a folder that has folders inside it.

12.6 Read links and load more mail

Some email has web-style formatting. Companion reads its links and buttons as part of the message, the same way as on the web. You hear "link" before a link's words, and "button" after a button's words.

  • K moves to the next link. B moves to the next button. Hold Shift to move back. Tab moves through both.
  • To hear every link, say "List links", or press F7. To hear every button, say "List buttons", or press F8.
  • Voice choices include "Next link", "Previous link", "Next button", and "Previous button".
  • To use the selected item, say "Activate current link" or "Activate current button", or press Enter.
  • To choose by number, say "Open link" or "Open button", followed by its number, such as "Open link 1".

A safe link opens in the browser you chose under Guided browser in Web browser options. An email address link starts a new email to that address. A button with no usable link is read, but it cannot run a script from the email.

A page of messages is a list with a start and an end. Up Arrow and Down Arrow stop at the first and last message. You hear "Start of list" or "End of list", and how to reach the next page.

  • For another page of mail, say "Next messages" or "Previous messages", or press Page Down or Page Up in the message list.
  • To check for new mail, say "List messages", or press F5.
  • To reach the first or last message on this page, say "First message" or "Last message", or press Home or End.
  • To reach the newest or oldest message in the whole folder, say "Newest message" or "Oldest message", or press Control+Home or Control+End. If Thunderbird takes a long time, press Escape to stop waiting.

Email letter keys work in the message list and in an open message:

  • N: to write a new email, say "New email", or press N.
  • R: to reply, say "Reply", or press R.
  • A: to reply to all, say "Reply all", or press A.
  • F: to forward, say "Forward", or press F.
  • M: to move to a folder, say "Move", or press M.
  • G: to choose a folder, say "Go to folder", or press G.
  • W: to hear the folder name, say "Which folder", or press W.
  • I: to hear the sender, subject, and date, say "Message details", or press I.
  • Alt+1 to Alt+6: to hear From, To, CC, Subject, the date sent, or how many files are attached. See part 12.1.
  • C: to copy the sender's address, say "Copy sender address", or press C.
  • E: to email the sender, say "New email to sender", or press E.
  • D: to open a saved draft, say "Open email draft", or press D.
  • P: to hear your contacts, say "List contacts", or press P.
  • S: to hear how many are marked, say "What is selected", or press S.
  • U: to remove all marks, say "Clear selection", or press U.
  • L: to hear the files sent with a message, say "What's attached" or "List attachments", or press L.
  • Space marks a message. Delete deletes. F2 renames a folder. F5 checks for new mail.

For email help, say "Help with email", or press F1.

12.7 Open and save attachments

When you open a message that has files, Companion says "Message opened." Then it says how many there are, with their names and sizes. For example, "2 attachments: report.pdf, 1.2 megabytes; photo.jpg, 300 kilobytes."

The Attachments list works the same for a message you got and for an email you are writing.

  1. To open the Attachments list, say "Attachments" or "What's attached", or press L. In an email you are writing, press Control+L. You hear, for example, "Attachments, 2 files. report.pdf, 1.2 megabytes, 1 of 2."
  2. Use Up Arrow and Down Arrow to move through the files. Or say "Next attachment" and "Previous attachment". Home and End go to the first and last file. A letter goes to the next file that starts with it.
  3. Press Enter to open the file you are on. Documents open in Document reader, and sound and video in the media player. Other files open in their Windows app.
  4. Press Escape to go back to the message or the draft.

Keys and voice commands in the Attachments list:

  • Enter: to open the file, say "Open attachment", or press Enter.
  • Control+S: to save the file in your Downloads folder, say "Save attachment", or press Control+S.
  • Control+D: to save it in another folder, say "Save attachment to folder", or press Control+D. Then say or type the folder.
  • Control+Shift+S: to save every file, say "Save all attachments", or press Control+Shift+S.
  • Control+I: to hear what kind of file it is, its size, and where it is, say "Read attachment details", or press Control+I.
  • Delete: in an email you are writing, to take the file off, say "Remove attachment", or press Delete.
  • Control+O: in an email you are writing, to attach another file, say "Attach a file", or press Control+O.
  • F1: for help in the list, say "Help with attachments", or press F1.

Press Alt to open the attachments menu. It has the same choices.

You can say a file's name the way you would say it:

  • To open a file, say "Open" and its name, such as "Open report" or "Open the PDF".
  • To save it in a folder, say "Save it to" and the folder, such as "Save it to Documents". You can name the file too, such as "Save report to Desktop".
  • To save every file in a folder, say "Save all to" and the folder, such as "Save all to Desktop".
  • The folder can be Documents, Desktop, Downloads, Pictures, Music, or Videos. You can also type a full folder path.
  • To hear the size of the file you are on, say "How big is it". For all the files, say "How big are they".
  • "Report dot PDF", "report", and "the report" all find report.pdf. You can also say the kind of file, such as "the PDF", "the photo", or "the spreadsheet". Or say a number, such as "Open the second one".
  • If two files fit what you said, Companion names them. Then say more of the name.

In an open message, you can say these without opening the list. For example, say "Open the photo", "Save report to Documents", or "Save all to Desktop".

  • Companion says where it saved the file. It never replaces a file that is already there. A second copy of report.pdf is saved as report (1).pdf.
  • If a folder cannot be used, Companion says so. Choose another folder, such as Desktop.

A program or script asks first before it opens. Only open programs from people you trust.

A large file can take a while. Companion says how far it has got. Press Escape to stop. Nothing half saved is left behind.

If a file is damaged, Companion says it could not be saved.

13. Contacts

14. Calendar

15. Browse The Web

15.1 Open the web browser menu

Choose Web browser on the main menu. The web browser menu opens, even if you used a page earlier. It has these choices:

  • Resume where you left off. This shows only when a page is kept open.
  • Search or enter address
  • Go to favorite
  • New favorite
  • Delete favorite
  • Rename favorite
  • Go back
  • Go forward
  • Web history
  • Downloads
  • Set up Chrome

Use the arrows and Enter, or say the name of a menu item. Press Escape to go back to the main menu.

On this menu, Alt reads the menu again.

15.2 Search or open an address

  1. Choose Search or enter address. From a page, you can also say "Search", or press Control+L.
  2. Say your search, or type it and press Enter. For example, type weather in Houston.
  3. Wait for the page title and site name.
  4. Use the page reading commands to explore the result.

To open a website, type its address, such as example.com. You do not need to type www or https.

By voice, say "Open website", followed by the address. Say "dot" for a period and "slash" for /. For example, say "Open website example dot com". Companion asks you to check the address before it opens the site.

You can also say "Go to", followed by the address, such as "Go to VA dot gov". In Search or enter address, just say the address.

Escape cancels the entry.

Google is the default search engine. You can also choose Bing, Brave Search, or DuckDuckGo.

  • To change the default, say "Search engine" or "Default search engine", followed by the name. For example, say "Search engine Brave Search" or "Default search engine Google".
  • To use another engine just once, say its name, then your search words, such as "Bing weather". Your default stays the same.

If a site asks you to prove you are human, Companion cannot solve that check. It may offer another search engine. Say "Try Brave Search", "Try Bing", or "Try DuckDuckGo" to repeat the search there.

15.3 Read a page

Companion reads the page in its own window. Use the page view in the Companion window for these keys.

  • Up Arrow and Down Arrow read blocks of page text. A block can be a paragraph, a list item, or text between links.
  • Left Arrow and Right Arrow read characters.
  • Control+Left Arrow and Control+Right Arrow read words.
  • Control+Up Arrow and Control+Down Arrow read paragraphs.
  • To reach the top or bottom, say "Top of page" or "Bottom of page", or press Control+Home or Control+End.
  • To reach the ends of the current line, say "Go to start of line" or "Go to end of line", or press Home or End.
  • To move a longer way, say "Page down" or "Page up", or press Page Down or Page Up.
  • To read from the top, say "Read page". For the next part, say "Continue reading", or press Down Arrow.
  • To hear the page name and the item you are on, say "Where am I".

Buttons and form fields say their names and types. A picture used as a button is read as a button. Checkboxes say whether they are checked. Password values are never read.

Reading follows the place you choose. After you move to a heading, the arrows go on from that heading.

When a website changes a form without a new page, the page view updates. For example, after an email step, you can move through the new password step. An old hidden field no longer works.

A new page may start reading by itself. To stop this, turn off Automatic page reading in Web browser options.

Press Alt to open the web page menu. It has Page, Lists, Favorites, and Options and help. The menu does not close the page. While you type in a page field, Alt does not open the menu.

15.4 Move to headings, links, or controls

Single letter keys move you around the page. Hold Shift with any of them to move back.

  • H: next heading. Numbers 1 to 6 go to a heading at that level.
  • K: next link.
  • B: next button.
  • E: next text box.
  • F: next form control of any kind.
  • X: next checkbox.
  • C: next choice box.
  • R or A: next radio button. A radio button picks one choice from a group.
  • T: next table.
  • L: next list. I: next list item.
  • D: next landmark or region. A landmark is a named page area, such as main content or navigation.
  • G: next picture.
  • M: next frame.
  • P: next paragraph.
  • S: next list box.

A heading that is also a link, such as a search result, says so after its level. For example: "JAWS downloads, heading level 3, link".

You can also move by voice. Say "Next heading", "Previous link", or "Next form control". To choose a heading level, say "Next heading level", followed by the number, such as "Next heading level two".

These keys move through the page. They do not type letters until you go into a text box.

Up Arrow on the first line says "Top of page". Down Arrow on the last line says "Bottom of page".

15.5 Open links and use lists

To open a link or use a button, move to it. Then say "Open current link" or "Activate current element", or press Enter. If it only changes the page you are on, you hear what changed, such as "Added to cart", and you stay where you were. A page dialog says "Dialog opened". When it closes, you are back on the button that opened it.

To find words on the page, say "Find", followed by the words, such as "Find login". Or press Control+F, type the words, and press Enter. If a link or button has those words, Companion offers it first. Listen to the choice before you use it. Otherwise it moves to the words in the page text and reads that line. Say the same Find again for the next place.

To see a list of items on the page:

  • For links, say "List links", or press F7 or Insert+F7. Links are listed in Tab order.
  • For headings, say "List headings", or press F6 or Insert+F6. Each heading says its level.
  • For form fields, say "List fields" or "List form fields", or press F8.

Use the arrows, Home, or End to choose an item, then press Enter. A heading moves your reading place there. A link opens its address. To go back to the page, say "Close list", or press Escape.

To find a name in a links or headings list, type its first letter. Type more letters quickly to narrow it. You can type the full name, with spaces. Backspace removes the last letter.

Type the same first letter again to move through matching names. The search stops at the last match. Pause before you start a new name. Typing only chooses an item. Press Enter to open it.

15.6 Enter or change a web answer

  1. Use Tab to reach the next control. Or press Up Arrow or Down Arrow to reach a text box. You can type as soon as you reach it.
  2. You can also press E or Shift+E to move to a text box and start typing at once.
  3. Type or speak the answer.
  4. To finish a one-line box, press Enter. This uses the site's main button, such as Next, Continue, or Log in. Or press Tab to keep the answer and move on. For a box with several lines, press Control+Enter.
  5. To cancel, press Escape. This goes back to page reading.

Tab keeps your entry and moves to the next control. Shift+Tab moves to the one before. Letter keys type text while you edit.

  • To keep your entry, say "Save field", or press Tab.
  • To keep the old value, say "Cancel field editing", or press Escape.
  • To select the text in the box, say "Select all". Say "Read selection" to check it, or "Scratch" to remove it.
  • For a checkbox, press Space, or say "Check checkbox", "Uncheck checkbox", or "Toggle checkbox".
  • In a choice box, the arrows change the choice. Home and End choose the first and last. You can also say "Next option", "Previous option", "First option", "Last option", or "Select option" followed by its name.

Some text boxes suggest answers as you type, such as the YouTube search box. Companion calls this an edit combo box.

  1. Type or say your words.
  2. Press Down Arrow or Up Arrow to hear each suggestion. Or say "Next suggestion" or "Previous suggestion".
  3. Press Enter to use the suggestion you heard, or the words you typed.

Escape closes the suggestions and keeps you in the box. Press Escape again to stop editing.

When the suggestions change, Companion says "New suggestions available". It waits until you stop typing, so you do not hear it for each letter.

In a box like this, say "Search for" followed by your words to search that website.

Read-only boxes cannot be changed. Passwords stay hidden in page text and in spoken review.

How text boxes, choice boxes, and sliders act:

  • Up Arrow, Down Arrow, or Tab onto any text box makes it ready. Just start typing. This includes search, email, password, number, and multi-line boxes, and boxes with suggestions.
  • The arrow keys leave a text box at its first or last line. Tab moves on to the next control.
  • When you arrow past a choice box or slider, Companion only reads it. Its value never changes by accident.
  • To use a choice box, slider, or spin button, press Enter. Tab onto it also starts using it.
  • On a tab, such as Privacy on a settings page, press Enter to choose it. You stay on the tab. Its part of the page comes after it.
  • On a switch, press Enter or Space to turn it on or off.

15.7 Fill in and review a form by voice

Say "Fill out this form". Companion asks for one answer at a time. With the keyboard, use Tab to reach each field and type each answer, as in part 15.6.

  • For a text question, speak the answer.
  • For a choice, say the option name.
  • For a checkbox, say Yes or No.
  • To leave an answer alone, say "Skip".
  • To stop the questions, say "Cancel form".

Some forms show more questions after an answer. Companion checks for them as you go.

To check your answers before you send:

  1. Say "Review and submit form". Or say "Review form" to hear the answers only.
  2. Say "Continue review" to hear the next part.
  3. If an answer is wrong, say "Change this field" or name the field. To pick it by number, say "Change field", followed by the number, such as "Change field two". Give the new answer, then review again.
  4. Say "Yes" only when all answers and the named button are right. "No" stops the submission.

With the keyboard, press F8 for the field list, or Tab through the fields.

If you change an answer after the review, the old question is canceled. Review again before you submit.

You can also press Enter or Space on a web button, or say "Activate current button". This uses the website's own checks, not the voice review. A button such as Next can move to another sign-in step. You hear "Loading page" when you use a button or link. If a required field is empty, Companion reads the website's message.

Some sites ask you to prove you are human. Use Tab and Shift+Tab to reach the check box and other controls. Press Enter or Space once, then wait for the result. Holding the key does not send more clicks.

The check box may clear while the site opens another challenge. Move through its controls with Tab. Use the site's accessibility option when offered. Companion reads the new controls as the page changes. The site decides whether you pass. If you cannot use the challenge, ask the site for help.

On a booking calendar, choose a service before a date. Some sites mark every date as No information until you choose a service. Press Space or Enter on the radio button, then wait for the dates to load. Dates the site turns off stay marked unavailable.

File uploads and some custom controls may need help. If a page cannot be read, do not guess at a final button. Say "Ask for help" to hear which page needs attention.

15.8 Read a web table and leave a page

To move to a table, say "Next table", or press T. Then use these keys:

  • Control+Alt+Up Arrow and Control+Alt+Down Arrow move by row.
  • Control+Alt+Left Arrow and Control+Alt+Right Arrow move by column.
  • Control+Alt+Home and Control+Alt+End reach the first and last columns.
  • Control+Alt+Page Up and Control+Alt+Page Down reach the first and last rows.

Voice choices include "Next row", "Previous column", "First row", "Last row", "First column", and "Last column".

Companion reads the cell text. It also reads the heading and the row and column numbers when it can.

To go back or forward a page:

  • To go back, say "Go back" or "Back", or press Alt+Left Arrow.
  • To go forward, say "Go forward" or "Forward", or press Alt+Right Arrow.

Companion says "Going back" at once. Then it says the page title and puts you where you were, such as on the link you followed. If there is no page to go back to, you hear "There is no previous page." Ads that change by themselves do not stop you from going back.

To reload the page, say "Refresh page", or press F5. For web help, say "Help with web", or press F1.

To leave the page:

  • Escape from page reading opens the web browser menu. If a text box, page dialog, or list is open, Escape closes that first.
  • "Leave website" also opens the web browser menu.
  • "Main menu" or Control+F1 leaves the web browser.
  • To close the browser window, say "Close browser window", or press Control+Shift+W. This works even when Keep browser window open is on, and from the web browser menu. Companion then goes to the main menu.

If you use your own Chrome with the extension, its tabs stay open. Companion just lets go of it.

15.9 Identify graphics, lists, regions, and frames

Companion uses the names that the website gives. A good name tells you what an item is or what it does. If a name is missing, Companion says the item is unlabeled. It does not guess what a picture shows.

  • Pictures: press G or Shift+G. Or say "Next graphic", "Previous graphic", "Next image", or "Previous image". Pictures marked as decoration are skipped.
  • Lists: press L or Shift+L. Or say "Next list" or "Previous list". You hear the list name, if it has one, and the number of items.
  • List items: press I or Shift+I. Or say "Next list item" or "Previous list item". A list inside a list has its own count.
  • Links: K and Shift+K move between links. L and I are for lists, even when the list holds links.
  • Regions: press D or Shift+D. Or say "Next region", "Previous region", "Next landmark", or "Previous landmark". A region is an area such as navigation, main content, or a named section. You hear its name and type.
  • Frames: press M or Shift+M. Or say "Next frame" or "Previous frame". A frame holds another page inside this page. You hear its title or label.

Frame text appears where the frame is on the page. Companion marks the end of the frame. Hidden frames are skipped.

15.10 Use website keys and sliders

Some websites have their own keys. For example, on YouTube, K plays or pauses a video. J and L skip back and ahead. M turns the sound off, and F fills the screen.

In browse mode, Companion uses letter keys to move through the page. To send your keys to the website instead, use app mode.

  • To use app mode, say "App mode" or "Focus mode", or press Insert+Space.
  • To go back, say "Browse mode", or press Insert+Space again.
  • JAWS, NVDA, and Narrator keep Insert+Space for themselves. If one of them is running, press Control+Shift+Space instead. Control+Alt+Space also still works.

When one of these screen readers is running, the first web page you open says which key to use. You hear this once each time Companion starts.

In app mode, every key goes straight to the website, even Escape and the arrows. This is true even when you type fast or hold an arrow key. Companion tells you where the website moves you and what changes. It also reads short messages from the website, such as Paused.

These keys keep their usual jobs in app mode: F5, F6, F7, Control+L, Alt+Left Arrow, and Alt+Right Arrow.

To leave the page from app mode, press Insert+Space or Control+Shift+Space first. Then press Escape for the web browser menu.

When you go back to browse mode, your reading place moves to where you were in app mode.

Each website keeps its own mode. When you go to a new website, Companion says which mode it uses. Leaving the page always goes back to browse mode.

A slider sets a level, such as the place in a video or the volume. Companion reads its name and value, such as "Seek slider, 1:23 of 4:05".

  1. To find a slider, say "Next slider", or press Tab until you reach it.
  2. If you reached it with Tab, you can use it at once. Otherwise, press Enter to start using it.
  3. Use Left Arrow and Right Arrow, or Up Arrow and Down Arrow, to change it. Page Up and Page Down make bigger changes. Home and End go to the start and the end. You hear the new value each time.
  4. Press Escape to stop and go back to browse mode. Tab also moves on to the next control.

16. Favorites And Web History

16.1 Save the current page

A favorite saves a page address so you can open it again later. Older website favorites are in the same list.

  1. Open the page you want to save.
  2. Say "New favorite", or choose New favorite in the web browser menu.
  3. Say a short name, or type the name and press Enter.
  4. Listen for the saved name.

If you started on the page, you go back to the page, at the same place. Escape also takes you back to the page, and nothing is saved.

A name can have up to 60 characters. If the name is already used, choose a new one.

To save with a name in one command, say "Save favorite as", followed by the name, such as "Save favorite as News".

16.2 Open or find a favorite

  • While you browse, say "Go to", followed by the saved name, such as "Go to My news". This works from the web browser menu too.
  • To open a favorite by name, you can also say "Open favorite", followed by the name, such as "Open favorite News".
  • To open the list, say "List favorites", or choose Go to favorite in the web browser menu. Use the arrows and Enter to choose.

To find a name in the list, press its first letter. Type several letters quickly for a closer match. For example, type n, then p, to find a name that starts with np. Pause before you start a new name.

You can also say enough of the name to pick it out. If two names match, say more or use the arrows.

A period added by speech recognition does not change which name you mean.

Home and End reach the first and last items. An empty list says it is empty. Escape goes back to the web browser menu.

If your chosen browser cannot start, Companion tries the other guided browser. Your browser choice and your favorites stay saved.

If a page cannot open, you stay in the web browser menu. Check your internet access, then try again.

  • Delayed phonetic pronunciation: adds a letter's spoken name after a short pause. For example, B is followed by Bravo. It is on at first. Choose a delay of 0.5, 1, or 2 seconds, or turn it off. Moving on or stopping speech cancels a hint that has not been read yet.
  • Echo dictated text: what you hear after you dictate. Choose phrase by phrase to hear each bit after a short pause. Choose at the end of a sentence to wait for a full stop. Choose do not echo dictated text for quiet speech entry. This does not change typing echo.

With at the end of a sentence, you can pause between phrases. Companion keeps them and reads the whole sentence once it ends. You hear the text without an Added label. A new task or a speech stop clears what is waiting.

To change these by voice, say the setting name and the value. For example, say "Verbosity medium", "Punctuation most", "Typing echo words", "Phonetics off", or "Phonetic delay 1 second".

When encountering capital letters sets how a single capital letter sounds while you type or read by character.

  • Choose raise pitch, lower pitch, play a sound, or do nothing. Do nothing is the default.
  • The sound is a short tone before the letter. The cue plays once when you reach or type the letter.
  • The delayed letter name uses your normal pitch and plays no tone.
  • To set it, open Options, then Verbosity. Tab to When encountering capital letters. Use the arrows to choose, then choose OK. Cancel keeps your old choice.
  • By voice, say "Capital letters play a sound", "Capital letters raise pitch", "Capital letters lower pitch", or "Capital letters do nothing". These save at once.

The choice is used in every part of the app, and is kept after you close it.

Letter names, such as Alpha after A, use your normal voice. Whole words and sentences keep their usual voice. With an AI voice, single letters use the local voice so you hear them quickly. Private password text is never read aloud.

25.4 Audio ducking

Audio ducking makes other sound quieter while Companion speaks. When Companion stops, the sound comes back up slowly. This works like audio ducking on an iPhone with VoiceOver.

Audio ducking is on at first. It lowers sound from these places:

  • Web pages in the guided browser, such as videos and music.
  • Chrome, Edge, Brave, and Firefox, even when you opened them yourself.
  • Radio and podcasts in Companion.

Other programs keep their sound unless you turn on Also lower other apps. Screen readers, such as NVDA, JAWS, and Narrator, are never lowered.

The sound stays low through short pauses between phrases. So it does not go up and down while you move through a list.

Ducking level sets how much quieter the sound gets. The choices are:

  • Light: 25 percent quieter.
  • Medium: 50 percent quieter.
  • Strong: 75 percent quieter. This is the default.

To change these settings with the keyboard:

  1. Press Alt+O to open Options.
  2. Choose Voice options.
  3. Tab to Audio ducking. Press Space to turn it on or off.
  4. Tab to Ducking level. Use the arrows to choose Light, Medium, or Strong.
  5. Tab to Also lower other apps. Press Space to turn it on or off.
  6. Choose OK to save. Cancel keeps your old choices.

To change these settings by voice, say one of these. They save at once.

  • "Audio ducking off" or "Turn audio ducking off".
  • "Audio ducking on" or "Turn audio ducking on".
  • "Ducking level light", "Ducking level medium", or "Ducking level strong".
  • "Ducking level 50 percent" also works.
  • "Lower other apps on" or "Lower other apps off".

If you close Companion while the sound is low, the sound comes back. If the computer or app stops all at once, the sound comes back the next time Companion starts.

If a program is still too quiet, open the Windows volume mixer and raise it there. Companion keeps your new level.

To choose, press Alt+O and choose Input options. Tab to Dictation type. Use the arrows to choose Old school or AI-based. Choose OK to save. Cancel keeps the old choice.

About AI-based dictation:

  • It works for speech in documents, notes, email, and web text boxes. Typed text and commands work as usual.
  • Names, passwords, codes, and web addresses are not cleaned up. Say "Type literally" before words you want kept just as spoken.

  • Text appears after a short pause at the end of each phrase. Speed depends on the PC. Keep phrases short.

29. Fix A Problem

29.1 Companion hears the wrong words

  • Speak one short sentence or command at a time. Wait for the reply before you speak again.
  • Check which microphone is chosen, and where it is. A headset helps keep the app from hearing its own speech.
  • Use "Commands only" while you review text. Switch to "Normal mode" before you write by voice.
  • If a command still fails, press Alt+T, type it, and press Enter. Listen to the result before you try again.
  • Do not repeat a send, move, or delete command if the result was uncertain. Check what happened first.

29.2 You cannot hear speech

  • Check the computer's sound output and volume.
  • Press Control to clear old speech, then give a new reading command.
  • If an AI voice is not working, say "Use SAPI". AI speech needs an internet connection and a working setup.
  • Check Speech volume and Speech rate in Voice options.
  • Ask your helper if you cannot hear any voice.

29.3 Email or a website will not work

  • For mail, check the account in use with "Which email account?". Ask your helper to check the sign-in if needed.
  • For a page, say "Refresh page", or press F5. If the layout is hard to explore, say "List links".
  • If letter keys type letters, press Escape to leave the text box. Use the Companion page window for its reading keys.
  • A site may ask you to prove you are human, or use a control Companion cannot read. Ask for help instead of guessing.
  • Some email folder changes are limited by the service. If an attachment will not send, it may be too large. Read the email chapters before you try again.

29.4 A guide or update will not open

  • Close any open guide files. Then say "Open user guides", or press Control+G, again. This asks Companion to refresh its guide copies.
  • If your Documents folder is not there, ask your helper to restore access to it.
  • If an update fails, keep using the current app if it opens. Try Check for updates again later.
  • Use the installed desktop icon. If it opens an older version, ask your helper to check the install.

When you ask for help, give the app version, what you tried, and the exact message you heard. This makes the problem easier to find.

29.5 Understand privacy and online services

Offline speech works on your computer. It does not send your microphone audio to a speech service.

Online services need your permission:

  • Online recognition needs your permission in Input options.
  • An AI voice needs your permission in Synthesizer options. Having a service key does not give permission on its own. Some older AI setups may ask you to confirm again.
  • To stop online narration, choose another voice, turn off Allow narration text to be sent to OpenAI, and save. Any AI speech still waiting is cleared.

The app keeps the documents, drafts, contacts, favorites, and history you use. Account sign-ins and AI keys are protected for your Windows user account. Documents and drafts are ordinary files, so protect your computer and your Windows account.

Email, websites, audio streams, contact sync, and update checks use the internet when you use them. Each service has its own data rules. The app has no user tracking or advertising.

The app skips history entries with private sign-in data. It also removes sign-in codes from older saved addresses. If a favorite needs you to sign in again, open the site and save its main page.

29.6 Keep a transcript of what Companion says

A transcript is a text file of everything Companion says. It also lists each key you press. Your helper can read it to see what you heard when something went wrong.

  • To start it, say "Start transcript". Or open Options, then Startup and exit options, and check Keep a transcript of everything Voice Companion says.
  • To stop it, say "Stop transcript", or clear the same check box.
  • The file is named speech-transcript.txt. It is in the Voice Companion data folder, where the app keeps your settings.
  • Passwords and other private boxes are never written. The file shows Hidden in their place.
  • When the file gets large, Companion starts a new one. It keeps one older file, named speech-transcript-old.txt.
  • While the transcript is on, Companion says so each time it starts.

  • To take one filter away, choose the first choice in its list, such as Any time.
  • To remove all filters, say "Clear filters", or choose Clear filters in the list.
  • Filters stay on for your next search until you clear them.
  • To load the list again, say "Refresh YouTube", or press F5.

YouTube no longer has a Last hour filter. It no longer sorts by upload date or by rating. If you ask for one of these, Companion tells you what to use instead.

31.3 Play and control a video

When you press Enter on a video, you hear "Loading video". Then the video plays and you are in the player. In the player, the keys control the video:

  • Up Arrow and Down Arrow change the volume.
  • Left Arrow and Right Arrow skip back and forward.
  • Space, K, or Enter plays and pauses.
  • N plays the next video, and P the one before.
  • Home starts the video again.
  • Escape goes back to the list you came from, such as your search results. The video keeps playing.

To come back to the player, press Enter on the video that is playing, or say "Player".

If YouTube says something went wrong, Companion loads the video again once by itself. If it still does not play, press Enter to try again, or choose another video.

The letter keys below also work in a YouTube list while a video plays. In the YouTube menu and the filter lists, letters move to the next name instead.

  • To play or pause, say "Play or pause video", or press Space or K.
  • To skip back, say "Skip back". To skip forward, say "Skip forward".
  • A skip is 10 seconds at first. To change it, say "Skip amount", then a time, such as "Skip amount 30 seconds".
  • To move once by a set time, say "Forward" or "Back", then the time, such as "Forward 2 minutes".
  • To start the video again, say "Restart video", or press 0.
  • To jump through the video, press a number key: 1 is "Jump to 10 percent", 2 is "Jump to 20 percent", 3 is "Jump to 30 percent", 4 is "Jump to 40 percent", 5 is "Jump to 50 percent", 6 is "Jump to 60 percent", 7 is "Jump to 70 percent", 8 is "Jump to 80 percent", and 9 is "Jump to 90 percent".
  • To change the volume, say "Video volume up" or "Video volume down". In a list, press Control+Up Arrow or Control+Down Arrow.
  • To turn the sound off or on, say "Mute or unmute", or press M.
  • To change the speed, say "Slower video" or "Faster video", or press Comma or Period.
  • To choose a speed, say "Video speed", then a number, such as "Video speed 1.5". "Normal speed" goes back.
  • To play the next video, say "Next video", or press N.
  • To play the video before it, say "Previous video", or press P.
  • To hear what is playing and where you are, say "What's playing?", or press F3.
  • To stop the video, say "Stop". To start it again, say "Resume".

"Faster", "Slower", and "Volume up" alone change Companion's voice, not the video.

In a list, Up Arrow and Down Arrow move through the list, even while a video plays.

31.4 Captions, quality, and chapters

  • To turn captions on or off, say "Captions on or off", or press C.
  • To hear the caption that is showing now, say "Read caption", or press R.
  • To choose the picture quality, say "Video quality", or press Q. Choose with the arrows, then press Enter.
  • To turn autoplay on or off, say "Autoplay on or off", or press A. With autoplay on, the next video starts by itself.
  • To make the video fill the screen, say "Video full screen", or press F. Press F again to go back. F11 still changes Companion's own window.
  • To list the chapters, say "List chapters", or press H. Press Enter on a chapter to go there.
  • To go to the next chapter, say "Next chapter", or press Control+Right Arrow.
  • To go to the chapter before, say "Previous chapter", or press Control+Left Arrow.

Some videos have no captions or chapters. Companion tells you so.

31.5 Ads

YouTube plays ads before and during some videos. Companion never hides an ad or skips it by itself.

  • When an ad starts, you hear "An ad is playing", and its number, such as Ad 1 of 2.
  • When you may skip it, you hear "Skip is ready". Then say "Skip ad", or press S.
  • When the ad ends, you hear the name of your video again.
  • You hear ad news only on the YouTube screen. On other screens, F3 tells you if an ad is playing.

Ads in the lists are named too. They start with the word Ad.

31.6 Learn about a video

  • To hear the title, channel, length, views, age, and likes, say "Video details", or press I.
  • To hear the description, say "Read description", or press D.
  • To read the comments, say "Read comments", or press O. Use the arrows to move between them.
  • To list the videos YouTube suggests next, say "Up next", or press X.
  • To see the channel's videos, say "Go to channel", or press G.
  • To use the real page on the web screen, say "Open in browser". To come back, say "Back to YouTube".

31.7 Use your YouTube account

Your likes, subscriptions, playlists, and history come from your own YouTube account. You sign in yourself, on Google's own page. Companion never types or keeps your password.

  • To sign in, choose Sign in in the YouTube menu, or say "Sign in". Google's sign-in page opens on the web screen. Type your email and password there. Then say "Back to YouTube".
  • To sign out, choose Sign out in the YouTube menu, or say "Sign out". Companion asks first. This signs the browser out of Google too.
  • Each change to your account asks first. Say Yes or No, or press Y or N.
  • To like the video, say "Like video", or press T. To take a like back, say "Remove like".
  • To subscribe to the channel, or to stop, say "Subscribe or unsubscribe", or press U.
  • To save the video, say "Save video", or press W. Choose Watch later or a playlist, then press Enter.
  • To save it straight to Watch later, say "Save to Watch later".
  • To post a comment, say "Post comment", then your words. You hear your comment first, and it is posted only if you say Yes. Everyone can see it.
  • Your own lists are in the YouTube menu. Subscriptions lists every channel you follow. New from subscriptions has their newest videos. History, Watch later, Liked videos, and My playlists are there too. You can also say their names.
  • The last item in the YouTube menu says Sign out when you are signed in, and Sign in when you are not.

When you are signed out, Home, search, and playing still work. Account actions tell you that you need to sign in, and how.

31.8 Sign in when you use embedded Edge

Google does not allow signing in inside embedded Edge. So in embedded Edge, YouTube works signed out.

When you ask for an account feature, Companion offers to switch to Chrome. Say Yes, and YouTube opens in Chrome, where you can sign in. Web pages will open in Chrome too. To go back, say "Use embedded Edge".

You can also use your own Chrome or Edge with the Voice Companion extension. You may already be signed in there. See chapter 18.

Google may also refuse to sign in a browser that a program controls. If Google says "This browser or app may not be secure", use your own browser with the extension.

31.9 Keys, menus, and other audio

  • Press Alt to open the YouTube actions menu. It has every action, with its key.
  • For help, say "Help with YouTube", or press F1.
  • When a video starts, radio, podcasts, and media files pause. If you start the radio again, the video pauses.
  • Control+P pauses or plays the video from any screen in Companion.
  • A video keeps playing when you go to the Media menu or another screen.
  • Going back to the main menu stops the video, unless Keep audio playing in the background is on. See part 25.8.
  • Videos play in a browser tab of their own, so you can look through lists while one plays.
