Guide version: 0.2.131-test.

Use this guide to learn Voice Companion, change its settings, and find help. Voice Companion speaks its own menus and messages. You can use your voice, your keyboard, or both. You do not need Microsoft Word or a screen reader to use its tools.

This is a test version. Some websites, email services, and audio streams may not work as expected. Read each message before you send, delete, or change anything important.

1. Getting Started

1.1 Install Voice Companion

Use the file named VoiceCompanion-Setup.exe. This is the installer. It puts the app and its guides on your computer.

  1. Open the installer.
  2. Follow its prompts.
  3. On the last page, leave Open Voice Companion now checked if you want to start the app.
  4. Choose Finish.

You can open the app later from its desktop icon or the Windows Start menu. Use the installed app, not an older copy from a download folder.

1.2 Open the app and wake it

  1. Open Voice Companion.
  2. Wait while it checks for updates.
  3. If it offers an update, say "Yes" or "No". You can also use the arrow keys and Enter.
  4. When it says it is ready, say "Wake up".
  5. Wait for the main menu. Say the name of the tool you want.

For example, say "Word processor", "Notepad", "Email", "Web browser", "Radio", or "Podcasts".

The keyboard works while voice commands are asleep. Press Down Arrow to hear a menu item. Press Enter to open it.

Say "What version?" to hear the version you are using.

1.3 Use the main menu

The main menu has these choices:

  • Word processor
  • Notepad
  • Email
  • Web browser
  • Radio
  • Podcasts
  • Options
  • Check for updates
  • Help
  • Shut down the computer
  • Restart the computer
  • Exit Voice Companion

With your voice, say "Next" or "Previous" to move. Say "That one", "Okay", or "Confirm" to open the item you hear.

From the keyboard, use Up Arrow and Down Arrow to move. Press Enter to open an item. Home selects the first item. End selects the last item.

You hear "Start of list" or "End of list" if you try to move past the first or last item. The list does not wrap around.

In most menus, press the first letter of a name to find a matching item. Press Enter to open it. Text boxes use those keys for typing instead.

1.4 Set up your microphone

Voice Companion uses the microphone selected in Windows. You can use a built-in microphone or a working headset.

A headset helps keep the app's speech out of the microphone. It can also make your own voice easier to hear.

If voice commands do not work, check the microphone in Windows sound settings. Check that apps can use it. Then close and reopen Companion.

You can keep using the keyboard if the microphone is not available.

2. Using Voice Commands

2.1 Give a command

Say a command, then wait for the reply. For example, say "Help" to open the guide.

In a menu, say "Next", then "That one" to choose the item you hear. Say "Main menu" to return to the start.

Names in examples are sample names. Replace them with your own document, contact, folder, or favorite name.

2.2 Speak text or give commands

In Normal mode, you can give commands and dictate text. In a document, draft, note, or web text box, ordinary speech adds text.

Say "Commands only" when you want to move around without adding text by mistake.

Say "Dictation only" when you want your speech to become text. In this mode, some words that are usually commands become text.

Say "Normal mode" to return to voice commands and text entry. Say "What mode?" to hear the current choice.

You can still leave a document, cancel a draft, or put Companion to sleep in dictation-only mode. Some editing commands also remain available.

If you are unsure which mode is active, switch to Normal mode before giving an editing or reading command.

2.3 Enter words that sound like commands

Say "Type literally", followed by the words you want to enter as text. For example, "Type literally new line" writes the words new line.

Without that prefix, "New line" makes a line break. "New paragraph" starts a new paragraph.

Say punctuation names while you speak. For example, "Hello comma Sam period" writes Hello, Sam.

Listen to the result. Speech recognition can choose the wrong word or punctuation mark.

The app treats typed words as text. Typing the word period does not add a period mark.

2.4 Type a voice command

  1. Press Alt+T to reach the command box.
  2. Type the command. For example, type list documents.
  3. Press Enter.

Use this path when you prefer the keyboard or a voice command is not heard correctly.

In a web page, Alt+T lets you type an app command. Press Escape to return to page reading.

2.5 Answer Yes or No

When Companion asks a question, listen to the item name and the action first.

Say "Yes" to do the action. Say "No" or "Cancel" to stop it.

From the keyboard, use arrows or Tab to choose Yes or No, then press Enter. Alt+Y answers Yes. Alt+N answers No. Escape cancels.

At a Yes or No prompt, Home selects Yes and End selects No.

3. Using Help And Lessons

3.1 Open a Help topic

Say "Help" to open the guide inside the app. Use "Next", "Previous", and "That one" to choose a topic.

You can go straight to a topic. For example, say "Help with documents", "Help with email", or "Help with web".

Other useful requests are "Help with radio", "Help with podcasts", "Help with presets", and "Help with commands".

From the keyboard, use the arrows and Enter to choose. Home and End reach the ends of a list.

3.2 Read part of a topic

Each Help topic has a list of smaller parts. Choose the part you want, then listen.

Help reads one complete paragraph or list item at a time. Say "Continue reading" for the next block. Say "Read from top" to start that part again.

Say "Next paragraph", "Previous paragraph", "Next sentence", or "Next word" to move through the text.

From the keyboard, press Down Arrow for the next paragraph or list item. Press Up Arrow for the previous block. Home goes to the first block. End goes to the last block. Long paragraphs are read in full, even when they wrap across several screen lines.

Choosing another Help item stops the old speech before the next item is read.

Press Control to silence speech. Say "Back to subsections" to return to the smaller parts.

Say "Back to topics" for the full topic list. Say "Close help" to return to your work.

3.3 Start the audio tutorial

The tutorial explains how to use the app. It waits for you between lessons. The tutorial explains the commands you give. It does not use them to send mail or change your files.

  1. Return to the main menu.
  2. Say "Start tutorial".
  3. Say a course name, such as "Email" or "Web browser". You can also use arrows and Enter.
  4. Listen to the first lesson.
  5. Say "Next lesson" when you are ready.

Courses cover Getting started, Word processor, Notepad, Email, Web browser, Radio, Podcasts, Options, and Help and updates.

3.4 Find, repeat, and leave a lesson

Say "Repeat" to hear the same lesson. Say "Previous lesson" to go back.

Say "Lesson list" for the lesson names. Use arrows and Enter to choose one.

Say "Find lesson" followed by a topic. For example, say "Find lesson favorites".

Say "List commands" for the course's command list. Say "Explain command", followed by the command you want to learn about. For example, say "Explain command send it".

If a long explanation pauses, say "Continue reading" to hear the next part. Say "Courses" to return to the course list.

Escape goes back one level. Say "Main menu" or "Close tutorial" to leave the tutorial.

4. Speech And Voices

4.1 Stop or pause speech

Press Control to silence Companion. This works while the microphone is asleep too.

By voice, say "Be quiet", "Stop talking", "Pause", or "Shut up". Say "Resume" or "Keep talking" to continue paused speech.

A new command can stop the old reading and do something else. For example, say "Next message" while mail is being read.

For audio such as radio or podcasts, use "Pause audio" or "Stop audio" to make your meaning clear.

4.2 Change speed, volume, or pitch

Say "Faster" or "Slower" to change speech speed. Say "Louder", "Quieter", or "Softer" to change volume.

To set a value, say "Rate" or "Speed", followed by a number. For example, say "Rate four" or "Speed four". For volume, say "Volume", followed by a number, such as "Volume fifty".

Speech rate runs from minus ten to ten. Zero is the normal speed. Volume runs from zero to one hundred.

Pitch changes how high or low a voice sounds. Say "Higher pitch" or "Lower pitch" to change it one step at a time. To set a value, say "Pitch", followed by a number. For example, say "Pitch minus three".

Pitch runs from minus ten to ten. Speech settings do not change text size or music volume.

From the keyboard, press Alt+O. Open Voice options. Use Tab to reach a setting, then use arrows to change it. Choose OK to save.

4.3 Choose a voice

  1. Say "Voice list" or "List voices".
  2. Choose the speech system you want. The list calls this a synthesizer.
  3. Choose a voice from that system.
  4. Listen to the sample and confirm your choice.

SAPI means the voices provided by Windows. eSpeak has US and UK English choices. AI voices need a separate setup and an internet connection.

Use "Next voice", "Previous voice", and "Okay" to choose. Say "Cancel voice" to keep your earlier choice.

The available voices depend on what is installed and set up on your computer.

4.4 Use an AI voice

An AI voice reads some app text through an online service. Your helper must set up access before you can use it.

Open Options, then Synthesizer options. Your helper can enter the service key in the private AI API key box. This key is a code that gives the app access to the speech service.

The Allow narration text to be sent to OpenAI checkbox lets you permit online speech. Check it only if you agree to send the text, then save the setting.

Say "Use AI voice" after setup. Say "Use SAPI" or "Use eSpeak" to use local speech instead.

An AI voice needs internet access and may use paid service credit. Long reading may take longer to start.

The app uses a local voice for typing and menu movement so you can hear each key promptly. If online speech fails, Companion uses local speech.

5. Reading And Editing Text

5.1 Read from your current place

These commands work in documents, email, web pages, and notes where text reading is available.

Say "Read from top" or "Start reading" to start at the beginning. Say "Continue reading" for the next part.

Say "Next character" or "Previous character" to move by letter or symbol.

Say "Next word" or "Previous word" to move by word.

Say "Next line" or "Previous line" to move by line.

Say "Next sentence" or "Previous sentence" to move by sentence.

Say "Next paragraph" or "Previous paragraph" to move by paragraph.

A line may end at a line break in the text. It may differ from a line shown on the screen.

5.2 Read with the keyboard

Left Arrow and Right Arrow move by character. Control+Left Arrow and Control+Right Arrow move by word.

In documents and email, Up Arrow and Down Arrow move by line. Control+Up Arrow and Control+Down Arrow move by paragraph.

In Help, Up Arrow reads the previous paragraph or list item. Down Arrow reads the next one. Home and End reach the first and last blocks in the topic.

Home and End reach the start or end of a line. Control+Home and Control+End reach the start or end of the text.

In web pages, Page Up and Page Down move a longer distance. Page reading starts from the place you last chose.

The same keys can review a received email. You can read and copy received text, but you cannot rewrite that message.

5.3 Select text

Selected text is the part you have chosen to copy, change, or delete.

From the keyboard, hold Shift while pressing a movement key. Control+A selects all the text in an open text view.

With your voice, say "Select word", "Select line", "Select sentence", "Select character", or "Select paragraph" for the current part.

Say "Select all" for all the text. Say "What is selected" or "Read selection" to check your choice.

In a document or draft, say "Select", followed by the part you want. You can use first, next, previous, last, current, or a number with character, word, line, sentence, or paragraph. For example, say "Select first paragraph", "Select next paragraph", "Select previous paragraph", or "Select paragraph 3".

In an email address box, a subject box, a note, or a web text box, selection applies only to the text in that area.

Web page reading text is read-only. To change a web answer, enter its text box first.

5.4 Choose named words

To select a word or phrase, say "Select", followed by the words you want. For example, say "Select red fox".

To select a passage, say "Select", followed by the start word, "through", and the end word. For example, say "Select cat through dog". You can use a phrase at either end, such as "Select red fox through sleeps".

You can also mark each end in a separate command. Say "Selection start at", followed by the start word or phrase. Then say "Selection end at", followed by the end word or phrase. For example, say "Selection start at cat", then "Selection end at dog".

If the words appear more than once, Companion reads each matching place. Say "Next" or "Previous" to hear another match.

Say "Read sentence" to hear the nearby sentence again. Say "That one", "Confirm that", or "Okay" to choose.

To choose a match by number, say "Match", followed by its number. For example, say "Match 3". You can also say "First match" or "Second match". Say "Cancel selection" to leave the text as it is.

To select the word all rather than all the text, say "Select the word all".

5.5 Copy, cut, paste, or remove text

Say "Copy selection", "Cut selection", or "Paste". From the keyboard, use Control+C, Control+X, or Control+V.

In a document or draft, say "Copy", "Cut", or "Delete", followed by the words you want to change. For example, say "Copy cat", "Cut cat", or "Delete cat".

Say "Delete word", "Delete line", "Delete sentence", or "Delete paragraph" to remove the current part.

Say "Scratch" or "Scratch that" to remove selected text. If no text is selected, the command removes the most recent dictated text where this feature is supported.

In documents and draft message bodies, say "Undo" to reverse an edit. Check the text after the change.

Moving to a new place clears the old selection. Check your choice again before you change or remove text.

5.6 Replace or insert words

To replace text, say "Replace", followed by the old words, "with", and the new words. For example, say "Replace cat with dog". You can also say "Change", followed by the old words, "to", and the new words, such as "Change cat to dog".

If the app finds one match, it changes that text at once. If it finds several matches, use "Next", "Previous", and "That one" to choose the right place.

Say "Cancel replacement" to stop without changing the words. Say a punctuation name if you want it in the new text.

For example, say "Replace cat with dog period" to use dog followed by a period. A period that speech recognition adds on its own does not become part of the replacement.

To replace the current word, sentence, or paragraph, say "Replace word with", "Replace sentence with", or "Replace paragraph with", followed by the new text. For example, say "Replace word with hello", "Replace sentence with Thank you", or "Replace paragraph with I will call tomorrow".

To choose where to add text, say "Insert before" or "Insert after", followed by a word or phrase already in the text. For example, say "Insert before red fox" or "Insert after red fox".

Choose a matching place if asked. Then speak the words to add. In Commands only, use "Type literally" before the new words.

6. Write A Document

6.1 Create and name a document

The Word processor creates files that Microsoft Word can open. You do not need to have Word installed.

  1. Say "Create a document" or choose Word processor.
  2. Say "Name document", followed by the file name you want. For example, say "Name document My Letter".
  3. Speak a short sentence or type into the document.
  4. Say "Read paragraph" to check what you wrote.

The file name and the title inside your letter are separate. Naming the file does not add a title to the page.

You can also press F2 to reach the name box. Say the name, then say "Okay", or type the name and press Enter.

6.2 Write and move through the document

Say "New paragraph" to start a new paragraph. Say "New line" to start a line in the same paragraph.

Say "Read paragraph", "Spell word", "Next word", or "Next sentence" to review your text.

Say "Go to beginning of document" or "Go to end of document" to move to an end.

You can also say "Move to beginning of document", "Move to end of document", "Jump to beginning of document", or "Jump to end of document".

After moving, new words are added at that place. A previous selection is cleared.

You can type directly too. Enter creates a paragraph. Use the reading and editing keys in chapter 5.

6.3 Change the look of text

Choose the text first if you want to change only part of it. With no selection, many commands change the current paragraph.

Say "Make this bold", "Make this italic", or "Underline this". Say "Remove bold", "Remove italic", or "Remove underline" to turn a style off.

To format specific words, say "Bold", "Italicize", "Underline", or "Highlight", followed by those words. For example, say "Bold cat", "Italicize cat", "Underline cat", or "Highlight cat".

To choose a font, say "Set font to", followed by its name. For example, say "Set font to Arial".

To choose a font size, say "Set font size to", followed by a number and "points". For example, say "Set font size to 14 points". You can also use the shorter form "Set size 14".

To set both, say "Set font", followed by the font name, the size, and "points". For example, say "Set font Times New Roman 16 points".

To change just the current word, say "Bold word", "Italic word", or "Underline word". To change its size, say "Set word size to", followed by the number and "points". For example, say "Set word size to 14 points".

To change the current word's font, say "Set word font to", followed by the font name. For example, say "Set word font to Arial". "Remove bold from word" turns bold off for that word.

Say "Say font" or "Read formatting" to hear the text settings. "Say font name", "Say size", "Say bold", "Say italic", and "Say underline" read one setting.

If selected text has more than one setting, Companion tells you that the setting is mixed.

6.4 Use headings and align text

A heading names a part of your document. Heading levels run from 1 to 6.

To apply a heading, say "Heading" or "Apply heading", followed by a level number. For example, say "Heading 1" or "Apply heading 1" for a main heading. Use a higher number for a smaller heading.

"Make this a heading" uses level 1. "Normal text" removes the heading style.

Say "Align left", "Align center", "Align right", or "Align justified" to place a paragraph on the page.

Justified text lines up with both page edges. Say "Say alignment" or "Say heading" to check the setting.

With selected text, these commands apply to the selected paragraphs.

6.5 Change line spacing

Line spacing is the space between the lines in a paragraph.

Say "Single space" or "Double space" to set the spacing. To give a value, say "Line spacing", followed by the number. For example, say "Line spacing 1.5".

For another value, say "Custom spacing". Give the value when asked, such as "One point seven five" or "Exactly eighteen points".

Companion repeats the value. Say "Yes" to use it, "No" to give another value, or "Cancel" to stop.

To change spacing throughout the document, say "Set document line spacing to", followed by the value. For example, say "Set document line spacing to 1.25".

Say "Read line spacing" for the current paragraph. Say "Read document line spacing" for the whole document.

"Reset line spacing" resets the current paragraph. "Reset document line spacing" resets all paragraphs.

A new paragraph keeps the spacing of the paragraph before it.

6.6 Make a list

  1. Say "Bulleted list" or "Numbered list".
  2. Speak or type the first item.
  3. Say "New paragraph" for the next item.
  4. Say "End list" when you want to return to ordinary text.

A bulleted list marks each item with a dot. A numbered list gives each item a number.

6.7 Make a table

  1. Say "Create table with", followed by the number of rows, "rows and", the number of columns, and "columns". For example, say "Create table with two rows and two columns".
  2. Speak the text for the first cell. A cell is one box in the table.
  3. Say "Next cell" to move to the next box.
  4. Say "Read table" to check your work.

You can also use "Previous cell", "Next row", "Previous row", "Next column", and "Previous column".

Say "Read cell" to hear the current cell. "Clear cell" removes its words.

"Add row" and "Add column" make the table larger. "Delete row" and "Delete column" remove those parts.

Say "Leave table" to write after the table. "Delete table" removes the table. Say "Undo" at once if that was a mistake.

The editor makes simple tables with straight rows and columns. It may not open more complex tables from other programs.

6.8 Save, copy, close, or reopen a document

Companion saves as you write. Say "Save document" to save under the current name.

To keep a second file, say "Save as" or "Save document as", followed by its new name. For example, say "Save as My Letter Copy" or "Save document as My Letter Copy". The first file stays in place.

To rename the file, say "Name document", followed by the new name. For example, say "Name document My Letter". Say "Undo" right after a rename to restore the earlier name.

Say "Leave document", "Exit document", or "Go back" when you are done. Companion asks whether to save.

Say "Yes" to keep the current file name, or to name an untitled file. Say "No" to discard changes made since you last opened, saved, or named the file.

Press Escape at the save or name prompt to keep editing. Check the file name Companion reads before leaving.

Say "Resume document" to return to the current document while Companion remains open.

After reopening the app, say "List documents", then choose a file. To open a file by name, say "Open document", followed by the saved name. For example, say "Open document My Letter".

Say "Open documents" to open the document folder in Windows. "Where are my documents?" reads its location.

Files are kept in Documents, inside the Voice Companion folder. They use the .docx file type.

Companion can reopen the simple files it creates. It may refuse a complex Word file to avoid changing it incorrectly.

7. Write A Note

7.1 Start and save a note

Notepad is for a short plain text note. Use the Word processor when you need headings, fonts, or tables.

  1. Say "Write a note" or choose Notepad.
  2. Speak your note.
  3. Say "Read from top" to listen to it.
  4. Say "Save note" to save it.

Say "Go back" to save the current note and return to the main menu. Going to sleep also saves the text you have entered.

7.2 Review or change a note

Use "Next word", "Previous word", "Next line", and "Previous line" to review.

Use "Next sentence", "Previous sentence", "Next paragraph", and "Previous paragraph" to move through longer text.

"Select all", "Select word", and "Select paragraph" choose text. "Read selection" checks it. "Scratch" removes selected text.

"Next character" and "Previous character" help you check how a word is spelled. "New line" and "New paragraph" add breaks.

Use "Commands only" to review without adding speech as text. Say "Normal mode" when you want to write again.

To enter words that sound like commands, say "Type literally", followed by those words. For example, say "Type literally new line" to write the words new line.

The same selection and editing rules apply to the current note. Keep a saved copy of any note you need.

8. Set Up Email

8.1 Connect an email account

Connect an account before relying on Companion to send or organize email.

  1. Say "Add account".
  2. Choose Gmail, Outlook or Exchange, or Yahoo as your email service.
  3. Follow the sign-in prompts. Ask your helper if you need help with the sign-in page.
  4. Open the inbox and send a test message to someone you know.

Google and Microsoft use their usual sign-in pages. Work or school accounts may need your organization's approval.

Yahoo requires an app password. You create this password in your Yahoo account settings for use with an email app.

Enter passwords and codes privately. Do not read them aloud to someone you do not trust.

If account setup is not available, ask your helper to check the app's email setup.

8.2 Choose another account

Say "Add another account" to connect a second address. Say "List email accounts" to hear the saved accounts.

To switch accounts, say "Switch to", followed by the email service name. For example, say "Switch to Yahoo" or "Switch to Outlook" if only one account of that type is connected.

With two accounts of the same type, say "Switch to" followed by the full address.

Say "Which email account?" to hear the account in use. Check it before sending mail.

"Check Gmail", "Check Outlook", and "Check Yahoo" open that account when exactly one of its type is connected.

8.3 Add and find contacts

A contact is a saved name and email address.

To save a contact, say "Add contact", followed by the person's name, "as", and their email address. For example, say "Add contact Bob as bob@example.com". Say "List contacts" to hear saved names.

To find a contact, say "Find contact", followed by the saved name. For example, say "Find contact Bob". Use the saved contact's name when Companion asks who to email.

Each email account has its own contact list. Local contacts can be used without internet access.

When Gmail is selected, say "Connect Google Contacts" or "Sync Google Contacts" to request access to Google Contacts.

Approve that access only if you want to link those contacts. A helper can assist with the permission page.

Google Calendar and Google Drive are not connected in this version.

9. Write An Email

9.1 Start a new message

  1. Say "Write an email" from the main menu, or "New message" while reading email.
  2. Give the address or a saved contact name when asked.
  3. Give the subject when asked.
  4. Speak the message in short parts. Listen back before sending.

From the keyboard in email, press Control+N to start a new message. Type an address in To and press Enter. Continue through the subject and message.

When speaking an email address, say "at" for @ and "dot" for each period. For example, say "sam at example dot com" for sam@example.com.

9.2 Change To, CC, BCC, or Subject

The To field holds the main recipient's address. CC sends a copy to another person. BCC sends a copy without showing that address to the other recipients.

Say "Go to To", "Go to CC", "Go to BCC", "Go to Subject", or "Go to Body" to reach a part of the draft.

Type the address or subject and press Enter. Left Arrow and Right Arrow review characters. Backspace and Delete remove characters.

To set the To field by voice, say "Email to", followed by the address. For example, say "Email to sam@example.com".

To set the subject, say "Subject is", followed by the subject text. For example, say "Subject is Hello from me".

Say "CC" or "BCC" to add a copy address. Say "Skip" if you do not want to add one.

"Read recipient", "Read subject", "Read CC", and "Read BCC" let you check those parts.

Tab moves to the next draft field. Shift+Tab moves to the previous field.

9.3 Write and format the message

Ordinary speech adds text to the message. "New paragraph" starts a paragraph. "Read paragraph" reads it back.

The text selection and formatting commands in chapters 5 and 6 work in the message body too.

You can change a font, text size, line spacing, heading, list, or table. Address and subject boxes use plain text.

The person reading your mail may see different fonts or spacing in their email app.

Use "Type literally" to write words that might be heard as commands. Use "Undo" to reverse an edit in the body.

9.4 Save or cancel a draft

Say "Save draft" to keep a message for later. Say "List email drafts" to find saved drafts.

To reopen a saved draft, say "Open email draft", followed by its name. For example, say "Open email draft Untitled" if that is the saved name.

Say "Cancel", "Cancel draft", "Go back", or "Back to inbox" to leave an unsent draft and discard its changes.

These cancel commands work in a new message, reply, reply-all message, or forward. They work from each draft field.

Save the draft first if you want to keep it. "Leave email" saves the draft and returns to the main menu.

Canceling cannot take back a message already sent. If you reopened a saved draft, canceling keeps its earlier saved copy.

9.5 Review and send

  1. Say "Send email", "Send it", or "Send that". From the keyboard, press Control+Enter in the draft.
  2. Listen to the sending account, addresses, subject, and message.
  3. If something is wrong, say "Cancel send". Fix it, then start the review again.
  4. Say "Yes" only when the review is right.

Nothing is sent before the review and confirmation. If no account is connected, Companion saves a draft instead of sending it.

After the email service accepts the message, Companion says it was sent. Check delivery with your helper during early tests.

If Companion says the result is uncertain, check your sent mail and ask the recipient before trying again.

10. Read And Organize Email

10.1 Read messages

  1. Say "Email" or "Check email" to open the chosen inbox.
  2. Listen to the sender, subject, and date of the selected message.
  3. Say "Next message" or "Previous message" to hear another summary.
  4. Say "That one" or "Open current message" to read its body.

To choose a message by number, say "Read message" or "Open message", followed by its number. For example, say "Read message 1" or "Open message 1".

Say "Message details" to repeat the sender, subject, and date. Say "Continue reading" for more of a long message.

Say "Read from beginning" to read it again. "Back to folder" or "Go back" returns to the message list.

From the keyboard, press Up Arrow or Down Arrow to choose a message. Enter opens it. Escape returns to the list.

10.2 Go to folders

Say "Go to folder" to hear a folder list. Use "Next", "Previous", and "That one" to choose.

To open a folder by name, say "Go to folder", "Open folder", or "Go to", followed by the folder name. For example, say "Go to folder Receipts", "Open folder Receipts", or "Go to Receipts".

Use "Back to inbox" to return to Inbox. "Which folder" tells you where you are.

To open a folder within another folder, give its full location. For example, say "Go to Projects slash 2026" or "2026 inside of Projects".

If two folders have the same name, listen to each full location. Choose the right one with "Next" and "That one".

From the keyboard, press Control+Y to open folders. Use arrows and Enter. Escape cancels the folder list.

10.3 Select, move, or delete messages

Say "Mark", "Select", "Mark message", or "Select message" to mark the message you hear.

Move to another summary and mark it too. "What is selected" reads the total. "Clear selection" removes the marks.

Say "Delete" to move marked messages to Trash. With no marks, it acts on the current message.

Say "Move" to choose a destination folder from a list. To name the folder directly, say "Move to", followed by its name. For example, say "Move to Saved".

"Delete message" and "Move message" act on the current message even if other messages are marked.

Listen to the action and names, then answer Yes or No. Check the folder before repeating an action with an uncertain result.

From the keyboard, press Space to mark or unmark a message. Shift+Up Arrow and Shift+Down Arrow extend the selection.

Control+A marks messages on the loaded page. Delete requests deletion. Control+Shift+V chooses a folder for moving mail.

Inside an open message, Control+A selects text to copy; it does not mark other messages.

10.4 Reply or forward

Open the message first. Say "Reply", "Reply all", or "Forward".

From the keyboard, press Control+R to reply, Control+Shift+R to reply to all, or Control+F to forward.

Reply all includes the sender and the other recipients listed on the message. Check the addresses before sending.

For a forward, enter the new address when asked. Speak your added text, then use the review and send steps in chapter 9.

Gmail and Yahoo forwarding do not include the original attachments in this version.

Say "Cancel" or "Back to inbox" to discard the response draft. Say "Save draft" first if you want to keep it.

10.5 Create and manage folders

To create a folder, say "Create folder", followed by the new name. For example, say "Create folder Receipts".

To create a folder within another folder, say "Create folder", the new name, "inside", and the parent folder name. For example, say "Create folder 2026 inside Receipts". Omit the parent name to choose it from a list, as in "Create folder 2026 inside".

To rename a folder, say "Rename folder" and choose it. Then say "Rename to", followed by the new name, such as "Rename to Bills". To give both names at once, say "Rename folder", the old name, "to", and the new name. For example, say "Rename folder Receipts to Bills".

Say "Move folder" to choose a folder and its new location. To give both names at once, say "Move folder", the folder name, "into", and the new parent folder name. For example, say "Move folder Bills into Archive".

Say "Delete folder" to choose from a list. To name the folder directly, say "Delete folder", followed by its name. For example, say "Delete folder Bills".

Listen to the full folder location and the action. Say "Yes" to go ahead or "No" to cancel.

You can delete only folders you created that contain no messages or other folders. Built-in folders such as Inbox are protected.

Some email services restrict folder changes. For example, Gmail labels with child labels have limits on renaming, moving, or deleting.

From the keyboard, press Control+Shift+E to create a folder, F2 to rename one, or Control+Shift+D to delete one.

10.6 Read links and load more mail

In email that keeps web-style formatting, Companion reads links and buttons as part of the message.

K moves to the next link. B moves to the next button. Hold Shift to move backward. Tab moves through both.

Press Enter to open a safe link. A button with no usable link is read but cannot run a script from the email.

Voice choices include "Next link", "Previous link", "Next button", "Previous button", "List links", and "List buttons".

Say "Activate current link" or "Activate current button" to use the selected item. To choose by number, say "Open link" or "Open button", followed by its number. For example, say "Open link 1" or "Open button 1".

Say "Next messages" or "Previous messages" for another page of mail. "List messages" or F5 refreshes the folder.

Home and End reach the first and last messages in the folder. Messages from another page may need to load first.

The Messages per list setting is in Email options. Choices include 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 100, 1000, and all.

11.2 Search or open an address

  1. Choose Search or type address.
  2. Say your search, or type it and press Enter. For example, type weather in Houston.
  3. Wait for the page title and site name.
  4. Use the page reading commands to explore the result.

To open a website, enter its address. For example, type example.com. You do not need to type www or https.

Say "Try Brave Search", "Try Bing", or "Try DuckDuckGo" to repeat the search there. Your default stays the same.

11.3 Read a page

Companion reads the page in its own window. Use the page view in the Companion window for the keys below.

Up Arrow and Down Arrow read blocks of page text, including paragraphs, list items, and text between links. Left Arrow and Right Arrow read characters.

Control+Left Arrow and Control+Right Arrow read words. Control+Up Arrow and Control+Down Arrow read paragraphs.

Control+Home and Control+End reach the page ends. Home and End reach the ends of the current line.

"Read page" starts at the top. "Continue reading" reads the next part.

The page view follows the place you choose. After you move to a heading, arrow reading continues from that heading.

A newly loaded page may start reading on its own. Turn off Automatic page reading in Web browser options if you prefer.

11.4 Move to headings, links, or controls

H moves to the next heading. Shift+H moves to the previous heading. K moves to the next link.

Use B for a button, X for a checkbox, C for a choice box, E for a text box, and F for any form control.

Use T for a table, R or A for a radio button, L for a list, and I for a list item.

Use D for a landmark, G for a picture, P for a paragraph, and S for a list box.

A landmark is a named page area, such as main content or navigation. A radio button chooses one option from a group.

Numbers 1 through 6 find headings at that level. Hold Shift with any quick key to move backward.

You can also move by voice. Say "Next heading", "Previous link", or "Next form control". To choose a heading level, say "Next heading level", followed by the number. For example, say "Next heading level two".

These keys move through the page. They do not type letters until you enter a text box for editing.

11.5 Open links and use lists

Move to a link or button, then press Enter. Say "Open current link" or "Activate current element" for the same task.

Say "Find login" or "Find contact us" to look for a matching item. Listen to the choice before you accept it.

F7 or Insert+F7 opens a link list. F6 or Insert+F6 opens a heading list.

You can also say "List links", "List headings", or "List fields".

Use arrows, Home, or End to choose an item, then press Enter. Press Escape or say "Close list" to return to the page.

11.6 Enter or change a web answer

  1. Use Tab to reach the next control, or E to find a text box.
  2. If you found a text box with E, press Enter or say "Edit current field" to start editing.
  3. Type or speak the answer.
  4. Press Enter to save a single-line answer. Use Control+Enter for a box with several lines.
  5. Press Escape to cancel editing and return to page reading.

Tab saves your entry and moves to the next control. Shift+Tab moves to the previous control. Letter keys enter text while you are editing.

You can say "Save field" to keep your entry. Say "Cancel field editing" to leave its earlier value unchanged.

Say "Select all" to select the text in the active box. Say "Read selection" to check it, or "Scratch" to remove it.

For a checkbox, press Space or say "Check checkbox", "Uncheck checkbox", or "Toggle checkbox".

In a choice box, arrows change the choice. Home and End choose the first and last options.

You can say "Next option", "Previous option", "First option", "Last option", or "Select option" followed by its name.

Read-only boxes cannot be changed. Passwords are hidden in the page text and spoken review.

11.7 Fill in and review a form by voice

Say "Fill out this form". Companion asks for one answer at a time.

Speak the answer to a text question. Use the option name for a choice. Use Yes or No for a checkbox.

Say "Skip" to leave an answer alone. Say "Cancel form" to stop the guided questions.

Some forms reveal more questions after an answer. Companion checks for them as you go.

Say "Review form" to hear the answers. Say "Review and submit form" to start the review before sending.

Say "Continue review" to hear the next part. If an answer is wrong, say "Change this field" or name the field.

To choose a field by number, say "Change field", followed by the number. For example, say "Change field two". Give the new answer, then review again.

Say "Yes" only when all answers and the named button are right. "No" stops the submission.

Changing an answer after review cancels the older Yes or No request. Review again before submitting.

You can press Enter or Space on a web Send or Submit button. This uses that website's own checks. It does not start the voice review.

File uploads, some custom controls, and human-verification checks may need help. If a page cannot be read, do not guess at a final button.

Say "Ask for help" to hear which page needs attention.

11.8 Read a web table and leave a page

12. Favorites And Web History

12.1 Save the current page

A favorite saves a page address so you can open it later. Older website favorites appear in the same saved list.

  1. Open the page you want to save.
  2. Say "New favorite". You can also choose it from the web browser menu.
  3. Say a short name, or type the name and press Enter.
  4. Listen for the saved name.

A name can have up to 60 characters. Choose a new name if the one you gave is already used.

To save with a name in one command, say "Save favorite as", followed by the name. For example, say "Save favorite as News".

12.2 Open or find a favorite

A period added by speech recognition does not change which saved name you mean.

While browsing, say "Go to", followed by the saved name. For example, say "Go to My news" to open that favorite. You can use this from the web browser menu too.

Choose Go to favorite. Use arrows and Enter to choose the saved page.

Press the first letter of a name to move among matching names. Type several letters promptly to choose a closer match.

For example, type n, then p, to find a name that starts with np. Pause before starting a different name.

You can also say enough of the name to identify it. If two names match, say more or use the arrows.

To open a favorite by name, say "Open favorite", followed by the saved name. For example, say "Open favorite News". Say "List favorites" to open the favorite list.

Home and End reach the first and last items. An empty list says it is empty. Escape returns to the web browser menu.

If your chosen browser cannot start, Companion tries the included Firefox browser. Your browser choice stays saved. Your favorites stay saved too.

If a page cannot open, you stay in the web browser menu. Check internet access, then try the favorite again.

12.3 Rename or delete a favorite

Choose Rename favorite, then select a name with arrows and Enter. Say the new name, or type it and press Enter.

Choose Delete favorite, then select a name with arrows and Enter. That removes the saved favorite.

Deleting a favorite does not delete the website or its contents. Escape cancels naming or closes the list.

In either list, type the first letter or several letters of a name to find it, just as you do when opening a favorite.

12.4 Open a page from history

Web history is a saved list of recent secure page addresses. It keeps up to 200 entries, newest first.

  1. Choose Web history in the web browser menu.
  2. Use arrows to hear page names, or type letters to find a name.
  3. Press Enter to open the page.

Your history remains saved when you close and reopen Companion. Back and Forward use the pages in the currently open browser session.

12.5 Resume where you left off

Open Options, then Web browser options. Check Keep browser window open if you want to keep a page for later.

When this setting is on, pressing Escape during page reading opens the web browser menu and leaves the page open.

Choose Resume where you left off to return to that page and your reading place.

When this setting is off, leaving page reading closes Companion's browser windows. The menu does not show Resume where you left off.

If the saved browser session has been closed elsewhere, Companion removes Resume where you left off from the menu.

Favorites and history remain saved with either setting.

17.2 Download and play an episode

Say "Download this episode" to save the selected episode for offline use. To choose by number, say "Download episode", followed by its number. For example, say "Download episode 1".

Say "List downloads" to hear saved files. Then say "Play download", followed by the file's number. For example, say "Play download 1". Files are kept in the Podcast Downloads folder.

The download limit is 500 MB for each episode. New searches and downloads need internet access.

Some podcast hosts do not allow downloads. If one fails, try another episode or ask your helper about the message.

17.3 Choose automatic downloads

A new subscription starts with manual downloads. It does not download every new episode on its own.

To set the number of episodes to keep for the open show, say "Auto download", the number, and "episodes". For example, say "Auto download 5 episodes" to keep the newest five.

To choose a show by number, add "for podcast", followed by its list number. For example, say "Auto download 5 episodes for podcast 2".

You can choose any whole number from 1 to 10,000. Say "Auto download all episodes" to save every episode offered by that show.

Saving every episode may use a lot of disk space. Companion pauses automatic downloads when disk space is low.

When you set a limit, the app removes older automatic downloads after new downloads succeed. It keeps the newest episodes up to your limit.

Files you saved with "Download episode" are not removed by this limit.

Say "Manual downloads" to stop future automatic downloads for that show. Files already saved remain in place.

You can also set the limit in Podcasts options. Keep Companion running for automatic checks and downloads.

17.4 Remove a subscription

Choose Remove selected subscription in the podcast actions list, or press Delete on a selected saved show.

Listen to the show name. Answer Yes to remove the subscription or No to keep it.

Check the message before agreeing so you do not remove the wrong show.

18. Change Your Options

18.1 Open, change, and save a setting

Say "Options" or press Alt+O. The app opens the Options menu.

  1. Use arrows to choose a category, then press Enter.
  2. Use Tab or Shift+Tab to reach a setting, OK, or Cancel.
  3. Use arrows to change a listed choice. Press Space to check or uncheck a box.
  4. Type in a text box when needed.
  5. Choose OK to save your changes. Choose Cancel or press Escape to leave them unchanged.

By voice, use "Next", "Previous", and "Okay" to open a category. Say the setting name and its value when supported.

Say "Cancel" or "Back" to return to categories without saving. Say "Close settings" to leave Options.

Opening another category or tool before choosing OK discards unsaved setting changes.

18.2 Choose how much speech you hear

In Verbosity options, you can choose how many hints the app reads. High gives full hints. Medium gives shorter hints. Low gives brief prompts.

These choices do not shorten a document, email, error message, or Yes or No question.

The Spoken punctuation setting controls which marks are read. Choices are none, some, most, and all.

The Typing echo setting controls what you hear while typing. Choose words, characters, characters and words, or none.

Delayed phonetic pronunciation adds a letter's spoken name after a short pause. For example, B is followed by Bravo.

It is on by default. Choose a delay of 0.5, 1, or 2 seconds, or turn it off.

To change a setting by voice, say its name, followed by the value you want. For example, say "Verbosity medium", "Punctuation most", "Typing echo words", "Phonetics off", or "Phonetic delay 1 second".

Moving again or stopping speech cancels a letter hint that has not yet been read.

18.3 Voice and Synthesizer options

In Voice options, you can change Voice pitch, Speech rate, and Speech volume. Check Lower media volume while Companion speaks if you want audio to become quieter while the app speaks.

Listen to the voice sample before saving rate, volume, or pitch. Control silences the sample. Escape cancels the category.

In Synthesizer options, you can choose SAPI, eSpeak, or an AI voice when available. This category also contains the private AI service key settings.

The checkbox Allow narration text to be sent to OpenAI controls consent for online speech. A blank key box keeps the saved key.

Check Remove saved AI key and choose OK to remove the saved key. Ask your helper if you need to set up an AI voice.

18.4 Input and Email options

In Input options, you can choose Normal, Commands only, or Dictation only. The choice affects how spoken words are used.

Allow microphone audio to be sent to Azure for recognition is off by default. Turn it on only if you agree to send audio and your helper has set up that service. It stays off in web forms and settings.

After changing this choice, say "Go to sleep", then "Wake up" to start a new voice session.

In Email options, you can choose plain text or HTML. HTML keeps formatting such as bold text and links.

The Sending account setting chooses the address used for new mail. The Messages per list setting controls how many summaries load at once.

The Announce email header names setting controls whether Companion says From, Subject, or another header name before its value.

In Spoken email headers, choose which details to hear: From, Subject, Date, or Size. Keep at least one header selected.

Press Space to include or remove a header. Alt+Up Arrow and Alt+Down Arrow change its place in the order.

Choose Add an email account to start the setup steps in chapter 8.

18.5 Word processor and Notepad options

In Word processor options, you can set the font, size, line spacing, and alignment for new documents.

These defaults do not change text in an existing document or draft. Font size choices run from 6 to 72 points.

Choose Open Documents folder to open your saved document folder in Windows.

In Notepad options, choose Open Notepad to return to your note or start a new one.

18.6 Web browser options

The Default search engine setting lets you choose Google, Bing, Brave Search, or DuckDuckGo.

Keep browser window open controls whether a page stays open when you leave page reading. It is off by default.

Automatic page reading controls whether new pages start reading aloud. It is on by default.

The Guided browser setting lets you choose Edge, Chrome, Brave, or Firefox. Companion uses a separate browser profile for its pages.

That means a site may ask you to sign in even if you already signed in in your usual browser.

Choose List website favorites to open the saved website list. New favorite actions are also in the web browser menu.

18.7 Radio and Podcasts options

Both categories have Keep audio playing in the background. It is off at first. With it on, audio keeps playing when you return to the main menu.

In Radio options, you can choose a Station database, List radio presets, or List scheduled recordings.

Station database chooses which station lists to search. Presets are saved stations. Scheduled recordings are future recordings.

In Podcasts options, choose a Podcast subscription, then set Automatic downloads for this subscription.

Choose manual, all, or a whole-number limit. List subscriptions opens the saved-show list.

The limit applies only to the chosen show.

18.8 Auto-update and Startup and exit options

In Auto-update options, you can turn Check automatically at startup on or off. Choose Check for updates now to check at any time.

In Startup and exit options, Start with Windows is off by default. Turn it on to open the installed app when you sign in to Windows.

The app starts in sleep mode and waits for "Wake up" or keyboard input. Uninstalling Companion removes that startup choice.

Ask before exiting Voice Companion, Ask before shutting down the computer, and Ask before restarting the computer are on by default.

Each setting controls its own Yes or No prompt. Document save questions still apply when you turn off an exit prompt.

19. Sleep, Exit, And Computer Power

19.1 Put the microphone to sleep

Say "Go to sleep" to pause ordinary voice requests. Say "Wake up" to continue.

Your place in a document, email, or menu is kept. A note or draft is saved before sleep where needed.

If an email is waiting for your approval to send, that approval is canceled. Review the message again before sending it.

The keyboard still works while voice commands are asleep. Sleep is different from closing the app or putting Windows to sleep.

19.2 Close Companion

Say "Exit Companion", "Close Companion", "Shut down Companion", or "Return to Windows" to close the app.

You can press Alt+F4 to request exit. Escape at the main menu also requests exit.

At the exit prompt, No is selected by default. Say "Yes" to exit or "No" to keep working.

If a document is open, answer its save question first. Escape cancels the exit request and keeps the document open.

Exiting Companion does not shut down Windows.

Companion says "Shutting down. Goodbye." and waits for the full message before closing.

19.3 Restart or shut down the computer

These choices affect the whole computer. Save your work in other apps first.

Choose Restart the computer or Shut down the computer on the main menu. You can also say "Restart computer" or "Shut down computer".

Companion asks a separate Yes or No question by default. Listen to the action, then answer.

If a document is open, Companion asks whether to save it first. Press Escape at that prompt to cancel the restart or shutdown.

Windows may ask about unsaved work in other apps. Companion does not force them to close.

The startup choice and the computer power choices are separate settings.

20. Check For Updates

20.1 Find a newer version

Choose Check for updates on the main menu, or say "Check for updates".

Companion also checks at startup when automatic checks are on. If an update is offered, say "Yes" to install or "No" to keep working.

A later update offer waits until you have returned to the main menu or a suitable pause in your work.

If the check fails, you can keep using the current version and try again later.

20.2 Install an update

  1. Accept the update with Yes.
  2. Let Companion save your current work and download the file.
  3. Listen to the progress messages. Say "Status" or "Update status" to hear the current percentage.
  4. Wait while Companion closes, installs the update, and opens again.

Say "Cancel update" while the download is in progress if you need to stop it.

The app checks the downloaded file before using it. Windows may still need your helper for a security prompt.

The app speaks progress at each five-percent step during download and installation. Say "Status" or "Update status" to hear just the current percentage. This works while a progress message is being read.

If Windows cannot open the microphone for the update window, the window stays open and keeps trying. Press the Status button or Enter to hear progress.

During installation, a separate update window appears. Its microphone listens for "Status" or "Update status". You can also press the Status button or Enter to hear progress.

When setup finishes, you hear "Installation complete, restarting Voice Companion." The full message plays before the app restarts. Companion opens in sleep mode. Say "Wake up" or use the keyboard when you are ready. If Windows takes time to release the microphone, the app tries to open it again.

Your account data and documents stay in their normal folders. The installer also refreshes the guides.

Companion does not force Windows to restart. It tells you if a restart is needed.

20.3 Read the release notes

Release notes explain what changed in a version and how to use the changes.

Say "What's new", "What is new", or "Release notes". You can also choose What's new in Help.

Use "Continue reading", "Next paragraph", "Previous paragraph", and the other reading commands to review them.

Say "Close help" to return to your task. The notes identify the current version and the earlier version used for comparison.

21. Find Your Guide Files

21.1 Open the guide folder

Say "Open user guides", "Open guides folder", or "Where are my user guides".

You can also use the Voice Companion User Guides shortcut on the desktop or in the Start menu.

Open Documents, then the Voice Companion folder, then User Guides. Opening the guide folder does not leave your current task in Companion.

After an update, close and reopen a guide that was already open so you see its new text.

21.2 Choose a guide format

Open the plain text copy in a text reader. Open the HTML copy in a browser to use its heading links.

Open the Word copy in Microsoft Word or another app that can read Word files. Open the EPUB copy in an ebook reader that supports that format.

The DAISY 3 copy contains text, not recorded speech. Unzip its files together, then open guide.opf in a compatible DAISY reader.

BRF is the braille-ready copy. It uses contracted Unified English Braille, with 40 cells per line and 25 lines per page.

The braille copy is made by software. It has not been checked by a human braille proofreader.

Each format has the same instructions. Release notes include the version in their file names. Older versioned notes can stay in the folder.

Updating the guides does not remove your personal files. The guides remain after you uninstall Companion.

22. Fix A Problem

22.1 Companion hears the wrong words

Speak one short sentence or command at a time. Wait for the reply before speaking again.

Check the microphone choice and its position. Try a headset so the app is less likely to hear its own speech.

Use "Commands only" while reviewing text. Switch to "Normal mode" before writing by voice.

If a command still fails, press Alt+T, type it, and press Enter. Read the result before trying again.

Do not repeat a send, move, or delete command if the result was uncertain. Check what happened first.

22.2 You cannot hear speech

Check the computer's sound output and volume. Press Control to clear old speech, then give a new reading command.

Try "Use SAPI" if an AI voice is unavailable. AI speech needs an internet connection and a working service setup.

Use Voice options to check Speech volume and Speech rate. Ask your helper if no voice can be heard.

22.3 Email or a website will not work

For mail, check the selected account with "Which email account?". Ask your helper to check sign-in if needed.

For a page, try "Refresh page" or F5. Use "List links" if its layout is hard to explore.

If quick keys type letters, press Escape to leave text editing. Use the Companion page window for its reading keys.

A site may ask for human verification or use a control Companion cannot read. Ask for help rather than guessing.

Some email folder changes and forwarded attachments are limited by the service or this version. Review the related chapters before trying again.

22.4 A guide or update will not open

Close any open guide files, then say "Open user guides" again. This asks Companion to refresh its guide copies.

If your Documents folder is not available, ask your helper to restore access to it.

For a failed update, keep using the current app if it opens. Try Check for updates again later.

Use the installed desktop icon. If it opens an older version, ask your helper to check the installation.

Tell your helper the app version, what you tried, and the exact error message. This makes the problem easier to find.

22.5 Understand privacy and online services

Offline speech works on your computer. It does not need to send your microphone audio to a speech service.

Online recognition needs your permission in Input options. An AI voice needs your permission in Synthesizer options. Having a service key does not grant permission on its own. Some older AI setups may need you to confirm permission again.

To stop online narration, choose another voice, turn off Allow narration text to be sent to OpenAI, and save. The app clears pending AI speech when you turn off permission.

The app keeps the documents, drafts, contacts, favorites, and history you use. Account tokens and AI keys are protected for your Windows user account. Documents and drafts remain local files, so protect your computer and Windows account.

Email, websites, audio streams, contacts sync, and update checks use the internet when you use those features. Service providers have their own data rules. The app does not include a user-tracking or advertising service.

The app skips history entries with private sign-in data. It also removes sign-in token values from older saved addresses. If a favorite needs sign-in again, open the site and save its main page.
