FREEDOMBOX DESIGN REFERENCE - SEPTEMBER 2026

This is a design reference, not a claim that Voice Companion already matches FreedomBox.

What historical sources establish:
- Serotek's original FreedomBox aimed at people who wanted email and the Internet without learning a complex screen reader. Mike Calvo described the first product as mainly voice-command driven and designed with limited dexterity in mind.
- A 2006 American Foundation for the Blind review describes a self-speaking desktop and Assistant, short numbered choices, a spoken heading when a new area opens, a way back, search, bookmarks, and extensive Help. The user could listen to choices and press Enter, or move through a list at their own pace.
- Its web forms did not require a separate forms mode; users moved to a field and entered text when told it was editable. Community Supported Access to the Web (CSAW) let users label otherwise inaccessible graphics for others.
- A later statement from FreedomBox's creators dates the self-voicing browser in FreedomBox 2.0 to 2004 and describes the goal as affordable, easy, portable, enjoyable Internet access.

Implications for David and Voice Companion:
1. Give a short spoken orientation on each new page: title, site, useful choices, and what he can say next. Keep the voice consistent and let him interrupt or request another chunk.
2. Let him state a goal in ordinary language. Offer a few relevant choices, describe each in human terms, and never require memorizing web element commands.
3. Keep an easy way back, favorites by simple names, and local help prompts. Explain every uncertain result and do not silently click a guessed target.
4. Ask one form question at a time, repeat the answer, and require a review before consequential actions. Do not speak passwords or codes. Banking and applications require site-by-site live validation.
5. Preserve a supported browser engine. A generic page-text extractor can miss unlabeled controls, dynamic pages, CAPTCHA, embedded frames, and rich forms; the app must admit that limitation and hand off to a helper.

Current implementation in this source: separate Edge session, controlled page readback, conversational link suggestions with yes/no, contextual spoken help, short-name favorites, simple guided text fields, guarded action confirmation, page-text excerpts with site attribution. These have mock-backed tests, not live Windows evidence. Natural language understanding is rule-based; no general AI service or model is configured. It cannot promise universal form completion, accurate research synthesis, or safe banking transactions.

Next engineering gates: Windows packaging of Playwright; live Edge launch; navigation, speech, and form tests across representative sites; handling frames and dynamic page changes; better answer grounding; a deliberately reviewed path for transactions and submission; a spoken in-app help experience; trials with David's speech and microphone.
